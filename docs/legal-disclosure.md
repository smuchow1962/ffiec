# Legal disclosure procedures

> **What this doc is.** The institution's posture when legal process compels disclosure of chain-of-custody secrets — master HMAC key, signing key, ledger contents. Closes the gap surfaced in fresh-batch simulation around court-ordered key disclosure.

## Court-ordered master-key disclosure

### What can be ordered

A court may order the institution to disclose:

- The master HMAC key (for adversarial verification of past chain integrity)
- The HSM-protected Ed25519 private signing key (rare; HSM extraction is structurally prevented for sign-only roles)
- The ledger contents (a discovery production)
- The session-key handshake records (custodian-side audit logs)

### Institution posture

For each disclosure type:

**Master HMAC key.** Disclosure is reasonable; the master is escrowable. The institution complies with the order, then immediately rotates the master. Past-event verification is unaffected because past signatures are HMAC under prior session keys derived at the time of capture; the master alone does not let an attacker forge past events without also having had access at the original time of capture. Going forward, the rotated master is the institution's new working key.

**HSM-protected signing key.** The signing key is non-extractable by HSM design. The institution cannot disclose what cannot be extracted. The institution can disclose the public key (already public via the tenant key registry) and arrange for adversarial verification to be performed against the public key. Courts familiar with HSM-protected keys typically accept this arrangement.

**Ledger contents.** Standard discovery production. The institution produces the ledger snapshot covering the relevant period, with the public key and tenant_id. The receiving party runs the verifier independently to confirm integrity.

**Session-key handshake records.** Audit logs of session-key issuance. Standard log production; the institution provides the relevant period.

### What does NOT change with disclosure

- Past-event verification still works against the disclosed master (the master derives session keys; the session keys produce HMAC tags; the tags match what was originally computed).
- The chain integrity property for past events is preserved.
- The verifier output for past events is unchanged.

### What DOES change

- Going forward (from the disclosure date), the institution treats all chain events as potentially repudiable until rotation completes, then the rotation establishes a new integrity boundary.
- The institution documents the disclosure in its control-evidence repository, including the court order, the date, the rotation procedure, and the institution's response.

## Subpoenas and discovery

### Discovery scope

For "all chain records relating to customer X":

1. The institution identifies the relevant `(tenant_id, run_id)` set using its customer-correlation index (institution-operated, CUEC).
2. The institution produces a ledger snapshot covering those runs plus the seal records that cover them.
3. The institution provides the tenant public key.
4. The institution provides any `key_fingerprint` records (per-entry stamps from the affected period) AND, where applicable, IKM-roster entries scoped to the disclosed runs so the receiving party can cross-check the looked-up IKM produces the recorded fingerprint and fully verify the chain.

### Discovery objections

The institution may object on standard grounds:

- Overbroad scope (request for "all events ever")
- Privacy / confidential business information (chain payloads may contain sensitive data; the institution requests protective order)
- Trade secret (the chain construction itself is open-source; no trade-secret claim)

### Production format

Standard discovery production formats:

- Native ledger snapshot (PostgreSQL dump, Parquet files for cold-store data)
- Verifier bundle (`verify --bundle` output) for the relevant period
- Public key and tenant_id (small files)

The receiving party reproduces the integrity verification using their own verifier instance; no need for the institution to certify the production beyond the standard authentication procedures.

## Federal Rules of Evidence considerations

### FRE 901 (Authentication)

To authenticate chain output as evidence:

1. The institution's records custodian declares the chain output was generated by the institution's verifier in the regular course of operations.
2. The verifier binary's SHA-256 hash is recorded in the bundle's `metadata.json`.
3. The ledger snapshot's SHA-256 hash is recorded.
4. The tenant public key's fingerprint is recorded.
5. The bundle's overall SHA-256 hash is the chain-of-custody-of-the-chain-output for the courtroom.

The institution maintains a records-custodian affidavit template covering these elements.

### FRE 902 (Self-authenticating documents)

Chain output may qualify as self-authenticating under FRE 902(13) (records generated by an electronic process or system) or 902(14) (data copied from electronic device or storage medium) if the institution provides the supporting certifications. Practically, the institution prepares a 902(11) custodian certification that:

- Names the records (verifier bundle, ledger snapshot)
- Confirms regular-course-of-business generation
- Identifies the responsible custodian
- Provides hashes and chain-of-custody pointers

The certification is signed under penalty of perjury per FRE 902(11) and is filed with the production.

### FRE 803(6) (Business records exception)

Chain records typically qualify as business records: kept in the regular course of business, by a person with knowledge, made at or near the time of the event, custodian testifies. The institution prepares records-custodian testimony covering:

- The chain records were kept in the regular course of business
- The records were made by a person (the SDK invoked by the AI agent process)
- The records were made at or near the time of the event (per `captured_at`)
- The records were verified by the verifier at the time of production

Business-records hearsay exception is generally accepted by courts familiar with electronic records.

## Cryptographic-expert testimony

For contested cases, the institution may need a cryptographic expert to testify to the chain's integrity properties. The spec is built for expert-testimony alignment:

- All primitives are standards-based (RFC 8785, RFC 6962, FIPS-approved algorithms)
- Design rationale is documented in `docs/design/`
- Threat model articulates adversaries and defenses
- Verifier source is available for expert review

Experts can describe the chain in court without proprietary-construct concerns. The institution maintains a list of qualified experts willing to testify, refreshed annually.

## Disclosure to regulators outside the bank's primary regulator

When a regulator outside the bank's primary regulator (state attorney general, foreign supervisor, U.S. Department of Justice) requests chain access:

1. The institution's legal counsel reviews the request
2. If lawful, the institution produces the same artifacts as for primary regulator examination (ledger snapshot, public key, tenant_id, verifier instructions)
3. The institution may negotiate a protective order if the chain content is sensitive
4. The institution does not disclose the master HMAC key or HSM signing key without a court order

## Privilege considerations

Chain content may include:

- Attorney-client privileged communications (an AI agent assisting with legal work)
- Work-product (an AI agent assisting with litigation strategy)
- Other privileged content

The institution's legal team identifies privileged content during discovery and may withhold it under privilege, with privilege log produced. The chain's integrity property does not waive privilege; it preserves the integrity of records that may include privileged material.

## Long-tail retention for litigation hold

When the institution receives a litigation hold, retention extends beyond the standard period for the affected scope. The chain accommodates extended retention:

- Cold-store retention is configurable
- Past public keys remain in the tenant key registry
- Past algorithms are recorded in the seal records
- Verifier remains compatible across spec versions

Long-tail retention scenarios (10+ years post-decision) are addressed in `docs/dr-and-resilience.md`.

## Operational checklist

When legal process arrives:

- [ ] Counsel reviews the request
- [ ] Records-custodian engaged
- [ ] Scope identified (specific runs, period, customer)
- [ ] Privilege screen if applicable
- [ ] Production prepared (ledger snapshot, verifier bundle, public key)
- [ ] Custodian affidavit prepared
- [ ] If master-key disclosure ordered: counsel arranges, rotation prepared, post-disclosure verification documented
- [ ] If HSM-key disclosure ordered: counsel responds with non-extractable explanation, public-key-based verification offered
- [ ] Production delivered with chain-of-custody documentation
- [ ] Disclosure recorded in institution's control-evidence repository
