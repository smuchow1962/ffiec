# Audit procedures

> **What this doc is.** Sample testing procedures for SOC and FFIEC examiners testing the institution's chain-of-custody controls. Closes the SOC and IT-examiner audit-procedures partials.

## How to use

For each control claim the institution makes, the SOC team or examiner needs a testing procedure that produces evidence. This doc lists representative procedures for the chain's controls. Engagement-specific procedures may diverge based on materiality, scope, and the institution's specific configuration.

## Procedures by control category

### Authentication and access (CC6.1, CUEC-IAM-01..05)

**P-1. Database role restrictions.**

- Pull the institution's database role grants
- Confirm the role used by the ledger writer has INSERT and SELECT only on events and daily_seals tables
- Confirm UPDATE, DELETE, and TRUNCATE are denied
- Sample-test by attempting an UPDATE under the role and confirming it fails

**P-2. HSM operator separation.**

- Pull the institution's HSM operator role list
- Confirm the seal-job operator role is separated from the HSM administrator role
- Confirm the seal-job role grants `sign` only (no extract, delete, import)
- For small institutions with dual-control compensating control: confirm the dual-control procedure is documented and operational

**P-3. Session-key handshake authentication.**

- Pull the IKM custodian's authentication logs for the period
- Confirm each handshake involved authenticated workload identity (SPIFFE / mTLS / HSM token)
- Confirm anonymous handshakes were rejected
- Confirm the per-tenant determinism property (spec §4.1.1 property 4) by one of two acceptable shapes:
  - **Self-test shape.** The institution operates a documented self-test that replays the handshake against a non-production IKM (or via an HSM dry-run mechanism) and records the resulting `key_fingerprint`. The SOC team inspects the self-test record and confirms the fingerprint matches the byte-identical value produced on prior runs.
  - **Sample-comparison shape.** The institution provides a non-production IKM the SOC team derives against directly (HKDF + fingerprint compute per spec §3); the resulting fingerprint MUST be byte-identical to any `key_fingerprint` previously stamped on a chain entry for the same `(tenant_id, key_version)` from the same non-production environment.

  In both shapes the comparison target is "any `key_fingerprint` previously stamped on a chain entry for the same `(tenant_id, key_version)`." The institution's procedure documentation MUST name which shape it operates and the SOC engagement working papers record the chosen shape per engagement.

  **Cadence.** The institution's documentation MUST specify the cadence at which the self-test (or sample-comparison) is operated. The minimum acceptable cadence is the lesser of (a) the institution's IKM rotation cadence, or (b) the institution's P-6 reconciliation cadence (no more than weekly per spec §10.1). The SOC team confirms the documented cadence was operated during the reporting period. An institution that runs the self-test once at SDK provisioning and never re-runs it is technically conformant on a single point but defeats the per-tenant determinism assurance over the period; the cadence floor closes that gap.

  **Sample-comparison non-production IKM provisioning.** When the sample-comparison shape is operated, the institution's procedure MUST document: (a) the non-production IKM's provenance — it MUST NOT be reused from production, even from a different tenant; (b) the secure transport mechanism to the SOC team — typical: a sealed envelope from the HSM custodian, opened in the SOC team's evidence room and shredded after the test, OR an HSM-mediated derivation path the SOC team exercises against the institution's HSM without the IKM ever leaving the device; (c) the working-paper retention policy for the IKM — the SOC team's working paper records the fingerprint and the test result; the IKM bytes themselves are NOT retained.

### Cryptography (CC6.7, CUEC-CRY-01..05)

**P-4. HSM PIN management.**

- Confirm the HSM PIN is sourced from secret manager (not embedded in config)
- Pull PIN rotation records for the period
- Confirm rotation occurred per documented cadence (typically quarterly)
- Confirm the previous PIN was invalidated post-rotation

**P-5. IKM custody.**

- Confirm the IKM is held in tenant-controlled storage at FIPS 140-2 L3 protection
- Confirm the IKM length is at least 32 bytes (spec §10.6)
- Confirm the IKM never appears on application hosts in plaintext form (no IKM files outside HSM/KMS; no IKM in process memory dumps for Model B deployments; for Model A, confirm `mlock`-equivalent or hardware-backed key handles per spec §4.1.1)
- Confirm the IKM is rotatable per documented procedure
- Confirm production builds do NOT include the software-key adapter (`kms_handle_uri = "plaintext-dev"` MUST NOT appear in any production chain entry; the build excludes the adapter at compile time per spec §10.7)

**P-6. Key-fingerprint reconciliation.**

- Pull `master.reconciliation_completed` events for the period
- Confirm reconciliation operated at the institution's documented cadence (default: weekly; spec §10.1)
- Confirm `fingerprint_unmatched_count` is zero or within the institution's baseline
- For each `(tenant_id, key_version)` pair observed in chain entries: verify the recorded `key_fingerprint` matches the recomputation `SHA-256(utf8(tenant_id) \|\| ikm)[:16]` against the IKM-roster's expected IKM bytes for that pair
- For non-zero unmatched fingerprints: review the institution's investigation. Common root causes: (a) botched rotation re-used `key_version=1` for a different IKM; (b) restored backup pointed at the wrong tenant row; (c) cross-tenant key swap from a vendor-side incident. All three are loud failures that the verifier catches before any MAC compute (spec §7 step 8); the reconciliation procedure provides the operational evidence trail.

### Operations (CC7.2, CC7.5, CUEC-OPS-01..05)

**P-7. Time synchronization.**

- Pull the institution's NTP-sync evidence (pollers, clock-drift metrics)
- Confirm application hosts and ledger servers are NTP-synchronized
- Sample-test by checking the time drift on a representative host

**P-8. Seal-age monitoring.**

- Pull `ledger_seal_age_seconds` metric for the period
- Confirm the metric remained within the institution's documented threshold
- For any threshold-exceedances: review the institution's response

**P-9. Notification on 72-hour seal delay.**

- Confirm any seal-job delays beyond 72 hours had documented regulator notification
- Pull the notification records (typically the institution's regulatory-affairs system)

**P-30. 60-minute seal-publication SLA test.**

Spec §4.3 makes seal publication a normative MUST: the signed root MUST be appended within 60 minutes of UTC midnight. P-8 tests the seal-age metric over the period and P-9 confirms the 72-hour notification posture, but neither directly tests the 60-minute MUST against the seal record itself. P-30 closes that gap by sampling against the `signed_at` field on each seal record.

- Pull the institution's `daily_seals` table for the period
- Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample across (a) day-of-week (sample at least 3 seals per weekday observed), (b) tenant (sample at least 5 seals per tenant), and (c) any seal events within 24 hours of an HSM-availability event so the SLA is tested under both nominal and stressed conditions
- For each sampled seal, compute `signed_at - day_boundary` where `day_boundary` is the UTC midnight at the end of the seal's covered day
- Confirm the delta is strictly less than 60 minutes for each sampled seal
- For any seal exceeding the 60-minute SLA: confirm the institution's response. The expected response includes (a) an operational event recording the SLA breach with root-cause classification (HSM unavailability, network partition, ledger-writer crash, scheduler misfire), (b) a documented investigation under IR Scenario 5 (seal-job degradation), and (c) confirmation that the seal-age metric reflected the breach in real time so downstream alerting fired
- A seal exceeding the 60-minute SLA without all three response elements is a normative-conformance finding (severity: High; the spec's MUST was missed and the institution's response did not produce the evidence trail the rework anticipated). A seal exceeding the SLA with the full response trail is a normative-conformance observation (the SLA was missed, the response was operated as designed; the SOC team records both the breach and the response)
- The 60-minute SLA and the 72-hour notification threshold are separate normative levels. P-30 tests the 60-minute MUST against the seal record's `signed_at` field; P-9 tests the 72-hour notification SHOULD against the institution's regulator-notification records. The two procedures consume the same `signed_at` data but answer different questions and produce different finding types

**P-10. Operational events log retention.**

- Confirm operational events from the period are retained per the institution's policy
- Confirm retention matches chain events retention (or longer)
- Sample-test by querying the log store for old events

### Verifier and independent assessment (CUEC-VER-01..04)

**P-11. Verifier-validation cadence.**

- Pull the institution's verifier-run logs
- Confirm validation script (`verifier-validate.sh`) was run before each verifier invocation
- Confirm cosign and GPG manifest validations passed

**P-12. Reproducible-build verification.**

- Confirm the institution performed at least one reproducible-build verification per verifier release
- Pull the institution's `reproducible-build-log` entries
- Confirm the rebuild matched the published binary

**P-13. Internal audit verifier-run cadence.**

- Confirm internal audit ran the verifier on the institution's documented cadence
- Pull the verifier output for the period
- Confirm a passing or pass-with-anomalies result; investigate any failures

### IR playbook (CUEC-IR-01..04)

**P-14. IR playbook documentation.**

- Confirm the institution has a documented IR playbook for chain-detected events
- Confirm the playbook addresses each scenario in `docs/incident-response-playbook.md`
- Confirm the playbook is current (reviewed within the period)

**P-15. IR playbook activation.**

- Pull any chain-detected incidents from the period
- Confirm each had a documented response per the playbook
- Confirm timing matched the playbook's response targets
- Confirm post-incident review was conducted

**P-16. Cyber-incident notification.**

- Confirm any reportable cyber-incidents during the period had timely regulator notification
- Pull the notification records
- Confirm the 36-hour FFIEC rule and any other applicable rule (CIRCIA, state) were satisfied

### Vendor management (CUEC-VND-01..04)

**P-17. Vendor SOC report consumption.**

- Confirm the institution received the vendor's SOC report for the period
- Confirm the institution reviewed the vendor's controls
- Confirm the institution identified any CUECs the vendor's report assumed institution operation

**P-18. Vendor IAM boundary (BYOC).**

- Confirm the vendor's image runs under bank-controlled IAM role
- Sample-test the IAM permissions; confirm the role grants only what's needed
- Confirm the vendor cannot reach the bank's HSM directly

**P-19. Container image trust path.**

- Confirm images are pulled through bank-controlled mirror registry
- Confirm cosign signatures are verified before deployment
- Sample-test by attempting to deploy an unsigned image; confirm rejection

### Anomaly evidence completeness (P-22 through P-25)

**P-22. `key_fingerprint mismatch` anomaly evidence completeness.**

- Pull all `key_fingerprint mismatch` (spec §7 step 8) anomalies from the verifier output for the period
- For each anomaly, pull the institution's anomaly-evaluation record per `docs/anomaly-documentation-template.md`
- Confirm the record contains all four required evidence pieces: (1) IKM-roster row identification — which `(tenant_id, key_version)` pair had an IKM that did not produce the recorded `key_fingerprint`; (2) change-management approval for the IKM-roster correction; (3) re-verification result on the corrected roster (PASS for the affected period, OR documented gap); (4) reconciliation cross-check — the institution's most recent `master.reconciliation_completed` event shows the corrected triple matching the IKM roster (`fingerprint_unmatched_count=0` for the affected pair)
- Any anomaly missing one or more of the four pieces is documented as an unmitigated control gap; the SOC team escalates per the engagement's standard finding workflow

**P-23. `hkdf_inputs_digest mismatch` anomaly evidence completeness.**

- Pull all `hkdf_inputs_digest mismatch` (spec §7 step 2) anomalies for the period
- For each, confirm the root cause is identified as a known SDK / verifier constants change documented in change management, OR the multi-region deployment-drift case where two production SDKs at the same `format_version` produced different digests for the same tenant (in which case the institution's response must include re-deployment to consistent constants and re-verification)
- Mismatches without one of the two acceptable explanations are escalated as format-construction defects under IR Scenario 1

**P-24. `audit_file_truncation_detected` anomaly evidence completeness.**

- Pull all `audit_file.truncation_detected` operational events for the period
- For each, confirm the writer-process crash is identified in the institution's host-level monitoring AND the recovery outcome is documented (`complete`, `partial`, or `unrecoverable`)
- For `complete` outcome: the dropped events are restored to the chain via the institution's documented recovery procedure
- For `partial` outcome: the recovered events are restored; the un-recovered subset is documented as an unrecoverable gap with IR Scenario 9 disposition for those specific events
- For `unrecoverable` outcome: the institution treats the gap as an integrity-control failure and starts the 36-hour clock per IR Scenario 9 notification guidance

**P-31. Truncation-rate baseline and Scenario 9 escalation threshold.**

P-24 tests evidence completeness for each individual `audit_file.truncation_detected` event. P-31 tests the institution's program for distinguishing baseline-noise truncations (normal SDK crash recovery in resource-constrained environments — edge fleets, dev-promotion gaps, transient host failures) from actionable signal that warrants IR Scenario 9 review. IR Scenario 9 starts the 36-hour clock only when "deliberate corruption" is suspected; without a baseline and an explicit threshold, the "deliberate corruption" determination is ad-hoc and the institution either over-escalates (a Scenario 9 review every week for routine crash recovery) or under-escalates (a real corruption signal lost in baseline noise).

- Pull the institution's `audit_file.truncation_detected` operational events for the period (spec §10.2). Apply the sampling table; stratify by environment (production, staging, edge fleet, dev) so baseline rates are measured per-environment rather than aggregated across environments with materially different operational profiles
- Confirm the institution maintains a documented rolling 90-day baseline truncation rate per environment. The baseline-tracking record is the audit-evidence shape — typical: a metrics-system dashboard or a control-evidence-event series the institution emits weekly. Confirm the record names the rate (truncations per million captured events, OR truncations per host-day, whichever the institution's monitoring expresses), the rolling window, and the per-environment breakdown
- Confirm the institution's documented alerting threshold for Scenario 9 escalation review. Typical posture: "truncation rate exceeding 2× the 90-day rolling baseline for the affected environment, sustained for at least 4 hours, triggers Scenario 9 escalation review." The specific multiplier and sustain-window are institution discretion; what the SOC team confirms is that the threshold is documented, quantitative, and tied to the baseline rather than a fixed magic number
- Confirm the institution reviews the threshold quarterly. Pull the most recent four threshold-review records. The review cadence ensures the baseline tracks the institution's actual operational profile rather than calcifying around a stale point estimate
- Confirm the threshold-crossing escalation path. Pull any threshold-crossing events from the period and confirm the institution's documented runbook was operated: (a) the crossing event was recorded, (b) the Scenario 9 escalation review was conducted within the documented response window, (c) the disposition (deliberate corruption suspected → 36-hour clock starts; baseline-noise driver identified → Scenario 9 closed with documented root cause; ambiguous → documented continued monitoring with re-review date) was recorded, (d) the disposition was reviewed by the institution's IR program owner
- An institution lacking the baseline, OR operating a fixed (non-baseline-relative) threshold, OR not reviewing the threshold quarterly, is a control-program finding (severity: Medium; the institution's IR program is operating Scenario 9 without the quantitative gating the playbook anticipates). An institution with the baseline + threshold + review cadence + escalation path operated as designed is a passing result regardless of whether any threshold-crossing occurred during the period

**P-32. Delayed-upload threshold testing.**

`edge-and-federated-ai.md` "Delayed-upload thresholds" names the institution-defined per-device-class upload cadence and the spec's 30-day default ceiling. P-32 confirms the institution operates the threshold and the escalation procedure as documented. The procedure is institution-side reconciliation against the chain-entry audit evidence; the chain's integrity property is unaffected by upload delays (the SDK chains locally; the verifier re-binds on ingest), but the audit-evidence question — "was delayed upload visible to the institution at the time it occurred, and did the institution respond per its documented procedure" — is the SOC and FFIEC examination shape.

- Pull a representative sample of chain entries from edge / federated / on-device deployments for the period. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample across (a) device class (intermittent-laptop, branch-office tooling, federated-learning node, disconnected-fleet, on-device — sample at least 10 entries per device class the institution operates), (b) `captured_at` distribution across the period (sample across early-period, mid-period, and late-period to detect temporal drift in the upload cadence), and (c) any periods that overlap a documented connectivity-loss event (sample at least 5 entries per documented event so the threshold is tested under both nominal and stressed conditions)
- For each sampled entry, compute `received_at - captured_at`. The two fields are the chain entry's load-bearing evidence shape per spec §4.4; both are persisted by the ledger per `design/06-ledger-server-design.md` and recorded by the verifier per `design/03-merkle-seal.md`
- Confirm the institution's CC8.1 control description names the expected upload-cadence per device class. Confirm the documented cadence aligns with the device class's operational profile
- Confirm the institution's documented escalation procedure for cadence breaches. The procedure names: (a) the device-class threshold (institution-defined; bounded above by the spec's 30-day default ceiling unless examiner-approved relaxation is on file), (b) the alerting mechanism that surfaces threshold breaches to the institution's IR program, (c) the disposition shape per breach severity (within-cadence: no action; cadence breach under 30-day ceiling: operational-variance observation; ceiling breach: examiner notification per the analog of `chain-of-custody-v1.md` §4.3.1)
- For each sampled entry whose computed gap exceeds the device class's documented threshold: confirm the institution's monitoring system surfaced the breach in real time AND the institution's response matches the documented escalation procedure for that severity. Pull the institution's per-entry alerting record OR the histogram dashboard's threshold-crossing record as the evidence
- For each sampled entry whose computed gap exceeds the 30-day default ceiling (OR the institution's examiner-approved alternative ceiling): confirm examiner notification was operated per the institution's policy. Pull the notification record from the institution's regulatory-affairs system. The 30-day ceiling is the spec-default failure-mode disposition; an unnotified ceiling-breach is a normative-conformance finding (severity: High; the spec's analog of §4.3.1 was missed and the institution's response did not produce the regulator-notification evidence the documented procedure requires)
- An institution lacking the per-device-class cadence documentation, OR operating without per-entry `received_at - captured_at` monitoring, OR not surfacing threshold breaches to the IR program, is a control-program finding (severity: Medium; the institution operates edge / federated / on-device deployments without the audit-evidence discipline the spec anticipates). An institution with documented cadences + per-entry monitoring + escalation procedure operated as designed is a passing result regardless of whether any threshold-crossing occurred during the period
- The 30-day default ceiling and the institution's per-device-class threshold are separate normative levels. P-32 tests the per-device-class threshold against the institution's documented procedure AND tests the 30-day default ceiling against the spec's failure-mode disposition. The two tests consume the same `(captured_at, received_at)` data but answer different questions and produce different finding types

**P-25. `gen_ai_parameters` schema completeness for SR 11-7 reproducibility.**

- Pull a representative sample of the institution's chain entries with `gen_ai_parameters` populated. **Sampling stratification:** the SOC team stratifies the sample by (a) AI model-version (sample at least 5 entries per distinct `gen_ai.response.model` value observed in the period; floor: 5 per stratum), (b) decision-class (sample at least 5 entries per `audit.*` event-class the institution operates — typical: routing, scoring, advisory, denial), and (c) customer-impact tier (sample at least 5 entries per institution-defined customer-impact tier — typical: low / medium / high). The 3 × 5 × 3 = 45-entry minimum stratification ensures coverage of the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface across the institution's actual decision pattern. **Fallback for institutions without a customer-impact-tier taxonomy:** smaller / regional banks may not yet operate a customer-impact-tier framework. In that case, the SOC team stratifies on (a) and (b) only and samples at least 7 entries per stratum (3 × 7 = 21-entry minimum); the SOC report notes the absence of customer-impact-tier stratification as a control-program-maturity observation, NOT a finding.
- For chain entries representing model calls: confirm `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` are present (per spec §4.4 RECOMMENDED guidance — required for SR 11-7 reproducibility)
- Confirm the institution's `gen_ai_parameters` schema covers the documented reproducibility surface: decoding parameters, sampler implementation identifier, system prompt content or content-hash with reference to a prompt-version registry, retrieval context (RAG document IDs and content-hashes), intra-run data dependencies (`(run_id, seq)` of earlier chain entries whose outputs fed this LLM call)
- Schema gaps are not chain-integrity findings (the chain integrity-binds whatever the institution puts in `gen_ai_parameters`); they are MRM-program findings that the institution's effective-challenge capability is weaker than the spec's reproducibility surface allows

**P-33. Routing-completeness sample testing.**

Spec §4.4.1 makes routing-decision capture a normative MUST for institutions operating multi-provider LLM deployments with failover, circuit breakers, or cost-routing. P-33 operationalizes the audit shape named in §4.4.1: it samples routing-event chain entries against the LLM-call chain entries they precede and confirms the linkage is complete. Routing-completeness gaps are a control-completeness finding (the chain decorator did not capture every routing decision the institution's router made), NOT a chain-integrity finding (the chain still cryptographically binds whatever was captured).

- Pull a representative sample of LLM-call chain entries (entries carrying `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model`) for the period. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). **Sampling stratification:** the SOC team stratifies the sample by (a) provider (sample at least 10 entries per distinct provider value observed in the period — typical: `openai-gpt-4o`, `anthropic-claude-sonnet`, `google-gemini-pro`, plus any institution-specific provider identifiers), and (b) period segment (sample across early-period, mid-period, and late-period to detect temporal drift in routing-capture coverage — typical floor: 5 entries per segment per provider). The stratification ensures the sample exercises the institution's routing policy across providers and across the period rather than concentrating on one provider or one window
- For each sampled LLM-call entry: identify the entry's `(run_id, near_seq)` — the LLM call's `run_id` and the chain `seq` immediately preceding the LLM call entry. Confirm the corresponding routing-event chain entry exists at that location AND that the routing entry's `audit.routing.provider_chosen` value matches the provider identifier resolved from the LLM call's `gen_ai.response.model` field. The linkage between the routing entry and the LLM-call entry uses `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` per spec §4.4 — the routing decision is the parent, the LLM call is the child
- A LLM-call entry without a corresponding routing entry at the expected `(run_id, near_seq)` is a control-completeness gap: the institution's router emitted the call but the chain decorator did not capture the routing decision. Likely root causes: (a) the chain decorator was not wired into the router (deployment misconfiguration); (b) the routing-event hook was disabled during a router-side change; (c) the OTel collector dropped the routing event between SDK and ledger. Route to IR Scenario 15 for the institution-side investigation
- A LLM-call entry whose corresponding routing entry's `audit.routing.provider_chosen` disagrees with the call's `gen_ai.response.model` is a different control-completeness finding: the routing decision was captured but the call landed at a different provider than the chain recorded. Likely root causes: provider-side reroute the institution did not record (vendor-relationship investigation), or chain-decorator timing race (the routing hook fired BEFORE the router's final selection)
- **Failover-event sub-sample.** Pull all `audit.routing.failover` chain entries for the period and apply a separate sampling pass (sample size: at least 25 failover entries OR all failover entries if the period total is < 25). For each sampled failover entry, walk forward in the same `run_id` and confirm the failover is followed by EITHER (a) another `audit.routing.attempt` entry that resolves to a successful `audit.routing.success` plus an LLM call (failover succeeded), OR (b) a terminating event with no LLM call within the same run (failover exhausted — the router gave up after the policy's last provider failed). A failover entry followed by neither shape is a control-completeness gap: the router moved away from a provider but the chain does not record what happened next. Route to IR Scenario 15
- **`failover_reason` discriminator (per spec §4.4.1).** The `audit.routing.failover_reason` enumeration distinguishes between provider-side and institution-side causes. The SOC team distinguishes between `rate_limit` (provider-side — the provider's API returned 429 or equivalent) and `quota_exhausted` (institution-side — the institution's per-tenant or per-customer-tier quota was exhausted before the call left the institution's perimeter). The two values branch differently: a sustained `rate_limit` pattern triggers vendor-relationship investigation (the institution's contracted rate limit is being hit); a sustained `quota_exhausted` pattern triggers institution-side capacity-planning review (the institution's quota allocation is undersized for its actual demand). `cost_threshold` is per-call cost guard (not aggregate quota); the working paper records the discriminator value verbatim so MRM-committee review and customer-dispute response branch on the documented spec enumeration rather than an institution-internal proxy
- **`audit.routing.refused` sub-sample (per spec §4.4.1 new event type).** Pull all `audit.routing.refused` chain entries for the period and apply a separate sampling pass (sample size: at least 25 refused entries OR all refused entries if the period total is < 25). For each sampled refused entry, confirm: (a) `audit.routing.refusal_reason` is present and one of the five enumerated values per spec §4.4.1 — `all_circuits_open` | `no_provider_in_policy` | `quota_exhausted` | `cost_threshold_at_capacity` | `policy_override`; a value outside the enumeration is a control-completeness gap (the institution's chain decorator emitted a non-conformant value); (b) the entry has NO `audit.routing.provider_chosen` and NO `audit.routing.failover_reason` (per spec §4.4.1 — neither attribute is present on `refused` because no provider was selected and no prior provider was being failed over from); (c) the absence of a child LLM-call entry within the same run after the refused entry — the chained refused entry IS the routing-decision evidence and the absence of a child LLM-call confirms no call followed. A refused entry followed by an LLM-call entry within the same run is a control-completeness gap: the chain recorded refusal but a call was launched anyway, indicating the chain decorator and the router's actual behavior disagree. Route to IR Scenario 15
- **Refusal-reason discriminator semantics.** The five refusal reasons branch into different institution-side investigations: `all_circuits_open` triggers a fleet-wide circuit-breaker review (every provider's circuit is open simultaneously, which usually indicates a shared upstream incident or a misconfigured circuit-breaker policy); `no_provider_in_policy` triggers a routing-policy review (the policy evaluation found no eligible provider for this request shape); `quota_exhausted` triggers institution-side capacity-planning review (same as the failover reason of the same name); `cost_threshold_at_capacity` triggers a cost-budget review (the institution's cost-routing budget for the window is exhausted); `policy_override` triggers a manual-override audit (the institution's policy explicitly precluded the call). The SOC team's working paper records the reason distribution per period so MRM-committee review and the institution's chain-operations team can detect systematic patterns
- **Evidence shape.** The audit evidence is the routing chain entries plus the LLM call entries plus the linkage via `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq`. The SOC team's working paper records, per sampled `(run_id, near_seq)` pair: the LLM call entry's `gen_ai.response.model`, the routing entry's `audit.routing.provider_chosen`, the routing entry's `audit.routing.event_type`, the routing entry's `audit.routing.policy_version`, and the linkage assertion result. For failover sub-sample entries, the working paper additionally records the resolution shape (succeeded / exhausted / unresolved)
- **Failure-mode disposition.** Routing-completeness gaps are control-completeness findings (severity: Medium when the gap rate is below the institution's documented threshold for routine operational variance; severity: High when the gap rate exceeds the threshold OR when the gap pattern correlates with specific providers, time windows, or router-policy versions in a way that suggests systematic rather than incidental loss). The findings are NOT chain-integrity findings — the chain's MAC + Merkle + HSM coverage of whatever was captured remains intact; the gap is in what was captured, not in how it was captured. The institution's CC8.1 control description names the institution's threshold for routine variance and the escalation procedure when gap rates exceed it
- **MRM committee connection.** The institution's MRM committee reviews the routing-completeness findings alongside the routing chain entries themselves per spec §4.4.1. Patterns the committee evaluates: (a) systematic shifts toward Provider B that correlate with Provider A circuit-state changes (policy functioning as designed); (b) systematic shifts with no observable circuit-state change (potential unrecorded vendor-side reroute warranting investigation); (c) gap-rate increases correlated with router-policy version transitions (chain decorator not updated to match the new router shape); (d) gap-rate increases correlated with specific deployment environments (deployment misconfiguration in the affected environment). The SOC team's P-33 working paper feeds the committee's quarterly review record alongside the standard MRM model-inventory and validation-schedule artifacts
- **Single-provider-deployment disposition.** An institution running a single-provider LLM deployment with no failover, no circuit breaker, and no cost-routing has no routing decisions to emit per spec §4.4.1. P-33 confirms the institution's CC8.1 control description names the deployment as single-provider and that the absence of routing chain entries is intentional rather than evidence of capture failure. The procedure's disposition for a confirmed single-provider deployment is "not applicable for the period"; if the institution adds a second provider during the period, P-33 applies from the date of multi-provider activation forward and the SOC team adjusts the sample accordingly

**P-26. Model-inventory composition cross-check.**

- Pull the per-model decision-count distribution from chain entries for the period. Aggregate by `gen_ai.response.model` and count distinct decisions per model.
- Pull the institution's MRM model inventory (the institution's MRM-program-defined list of AI models in scope under SR 11-7).
- Cross-check:

**P-34. `received_at` storage-integrity sample.**

Spec §4.2.2 names the ledger's `received_at` field as institution-trusted ledger-side metadata for day-boundary partitioning. The field is NOT part of the SDK's at-capture canonical bytes (binding it under the SDK's MAC would require the SDK to know its own future ingest timestamp, which is operationally impossible). The chain's MAC covers the SDK's at-capture content; the Merkle seal covers the ledger's day-aggregated content; the institution's append-only storage controls cover the ledger's post-ingest retention — three independent evidence layers. P-34 tests the third layer: the operational control that prevents post-ingest mutation of `received_at`. A ledger that mutates `received_at` after ingest is a control failure independent of the chain's cryptographic integrity, and the failure routes to a control-completeness finding (NOT a chain-integrity finding).

- Pull a representative sample of chain-event records from the institution's ledger storage for the period. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample by (a) tenant (sample at least 10 events per tenant), (b) `received_at` distribution across the period (sample across early-period, mid-period, and late-period to detect temporal patterns), and (c) any windows that overlap a ledger-storage configuration change so the integrity property is tested under both nominal and changed conditions
- For each sampled event, confirm the ledger's stored `received_at` value has not been mutated post-ingest. Acceptable evidence shapes: (a) the institution's append-only enforcement evidence (database role grants confirming UPDATE and DELETE are denied on the events table per spec §10.3 and audit-procedure P-1); (b) the institution's WORM or version-controlled storage configuration covering `received_at` columns (or equivalent storage-side immutability evidence); (c) the institution's ledger audit-log records covering any administrative access to the storage during the period (a non-empty admin-access window prompts targeted investigation of whether `received_at` was within the access scope); (d) cross-checking the sampled event's `received_at` against any independent record of the value at ingest time (the ledger's WAL, the institution's monitoring-system snapshot of ingest events, or the event's downstream copy in the institution's observability stack)
- Confirm the institution's CC8.1 control description names the append-only storage posture for `received_at` and the operational controls preventing post-ingest rewriting. The control description references spec §4.2.2 and §10.3 explicitly so the SOC team and the FFIEC examiner have a documented anchor
- For any sampled event whose `received_at` evidence is incomplete or inconsistent: route to a control-completeness finding (NOT a chain-integrity finding). The chain's MAC and Merkle seal cover the at-capture and at-aggregation integrity claims; the `received_at` storage-integrity claim is a separate operational control and a gap there does not break chain integrity. The institution's response includes documenting the storage-control gap, restoring the append-only posture, and re-running the sample on the corrected configuration
- An institution lacking the append-only enforcement on `received_at`, OR operating without administrative-access logging, OR not retaining ingest-time records for cross-check, is a control-program finding (severity: Medium when the gap is documented and bounded; severity: High when the gap is undocumented or the institution cannot demonstrate post-ingest integrity for the period). The finding is control-completeness, not chain-integrity — the cryptographic chain remains intact regardless

**P-36. Chain-completeness affirmative claim.**

The chain proves what the institution captured. It does not, by itself, prove what the institution failed to capture. A customer or litigant can argue "the AI made a decision on my application that you did not record" — the institution's response relies on the chain's completeness. That response is only credible if the institution's completeness claim was on record before the dispute arose. P-36 confirms the institution operates the completeness program the litigation defense depends on.

- Confirm the institution's CC8.1 control description names a decision-completeness claim. Acceptable shapes: "every AI decision on a credit application is captured in the chain"; "all decisions flagged as fraud-related are captured"; "all advisory-class outputs delivered to a human user are captured." The claim is scoped — the institution names the decision class it claims complete, not "everything the AI ever does." A claim too broad to operate is a control-program finding (severity: Medium); the institution either narrows the claim to what it can substantiate or strengthens its capture coverage to match the broader claim.
- Confirm the institution's chain-operations team operates a periodic completeness check correlating expected decision count (from the upstream system that drives AI invocations) against chain entry count. The check produces a per-period reconciliation: expected = N decisions per the upstream system's decision log; observed = M chain entries with matching `audit.*` event-class. The reconciliation cadence is institution discretion (typically daily or weekly); the SOC team confirms the cadence is documented and operated.
- Confirm discrepancies are documented and routed to a control-completeness finding. A discrepancy where N > M means the chain missed decisions the institution claimed it captured — the gap is investigated, the root cause documented, and the affected period's completeness claim either repaired (the missing entries are recovered) or scoped out (the institution documents the gap and its boundary). A discrepancy where N < M means the chain captured more entries than the upstream system records as decisions — typically a duplicate-emission bug or a chain entry not corresponding to a real decision.
- The completeness claim is the institution's litigation defense for negative-evidence assertions. The argument is: "The absence of a chain entry for application X is admissible proof the AI did not decide on application X." The argument is only valid if the completeness claim is on record before the dispute arises and the institution's reconciliation evidence supports the claim across the period. An ad-hoc completeness assertion made for the first time during litigation is much weaker than a documented control with months of operating evidence.
- Cross-reference: `docs/litigation-support.md` (negative-evidence claims, completeness as litigation defense), `customer-dispute-procedures.md` §"Adverse-action notices (ECOA)" (the customer-dispute disposition path that consumes the completeness claim), spec §4.4 (the chain entry shape the completeness claim is scoped against).

**P-38. OTLP severity filter audit (collector and receiver).**

Spec §4.4.3 and §4.4.4 make transport-identification and severity-handling for chain-of-custody traffic normative. The collector configuration MUST exempt chain traffic from severity filters; the receiver MUST stamp received chain records with a non-default `SeverityNumber` so downstream filters that have not been updated for the pass-through rule still admit chain traffic by default. P-38 samples the institution's collector and receiver configurations against these requirements. A finding of severity-based filtering on chain traffic is a control-completeness gap (the institution's pipeline drops chain records before the verifier sees them; the chain verifies what arrived, not what was dropped), NOT a chain-integrity finding (the chain's MAC + Merkle + HSM coverage of whatever was captured remains intact).

- Pull a representative sample of (collector configuration, receiver configuration) pairs for the period. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample by (a) deployment environment (production, staging, edge fleet — sample at least 3 pairs per environment the institution operates), (b) collector version (sample across collector versions deployed during the period to detect drift after collector upgrades), and (c) any windows that overlap a documented collector or receiver configuration change so the rule is tested under both nominal and changed conditions
- For each sampled collector configuration, confirm chain-of-custody traffic is exempted from severity filters. Identification at the collector layer is by Resource attribute `ffiec.chain.spec` per spec §4.4.3. The expected pattern is a routing connector (or equivalent) that splits chain traffic into a dedicated pipeline branch with NO `filter/severity` processor and NO severity-based `tail_sampling` policy. Confirm the chain pipeline's processor list does not include any processor whose configuration filters on `severity_number` or `severity_text`. A collector configuration that applies severity filters to records carrying `ffiec.chain.spec` is a control-completeness gap
- For each sampled collector configuration, confirm no severity-based sampling rule applies to the chain-of-custody pipeline branch. Sampling processors that drop traffic based on severity (the OTel `tail_sampling` processor with severity policies, vendor-specific severity-based samplers, or any processor whose configuration references `severity_number` or `severity_text` in a sampling decision) MUST NOT appear in the chain pipeline. The chain pipeline applies batching only — sampling processors in the chain branch produce a control-completeness gap regardless of the actual sampling rate observed
- For each sampled receiver configuration, confirm the receiver stamps a non-default `SeverityNumber` on received chain records. The Herald.Compliance reference uses its `QuickLogBuilder` resolver to position the level dynamically per ingest within the spec's normative range `9 ≤ N ≤ 20` (INFO floor through ERROR4 ceiling, just below `FATAL = 21`). The reference `SeverityText` is `"OTLP"`. Institution-specific stampings are acceptable when documented in CC8.1 — the institution's CC8.1 control description names the resolver mechanism, the produced `SeverityNumber` range under the institution's policy, and the `SeverityText` value, and the SOC team confirms the receiver configuration matches the documented stamping. A receiver that leaves chain records at the default `SeverityNumber = 9` (INFO) without resolver positioning, or stamps outside the `9..20` range (in particular at TRACE 1-4, DEBUG 5-8, or FATAL 21-24), is a control-completeness gap because routine severity filters downstream from the receiver may drop the records silently or routine alerting infrastructure may misinterpret them
- For each sampled receiver configuration, confirm any downstream filters or aggregations within Herald.Compliance (or the institution's equivalent receiver-side processing) exempt records with the institution's documented chain `SeverityText` from filtering and aggregation. The expected pattern is filter or aggregation rules that explicitly include `SeverityText != "OTLP"` (or the institution's equivalent) as a precondition. A receiver that stamps the chain `SeverityText` correctly but applies a downstream filter that drops chain records anyway is a control-completeness gap
- **Failure-mode disposition.** A finding of severity-based filtering on chain traffic at the collector OR a finding of receiver-side filtering or aggregation that drops chain records is a control-completeness gap routed per the institution's CC8.1 procedure. Severity is Medium when the gap is bounded (a single environment, a single collector version, or a documented configuration error) and the institution's response includes restoring the pass-through posture and re-running the sample on the corrected configuration. Severity is High when the gap is unbounded (the institution cannot enumerate which chain records were dropped during the period) OR when the gap pattern correlates with a verifier-reported chain integrity gap (a chain entry the verifier expected at `seq N` is missing, and the collector or receiver dropped it). The findings are NOT chain-integrity findings on their own — the chain's cryptographic coverage of what was captured remains intact; the gap is in what reached the ledger
- An institution that does not operate an OTel collector (the SDK exports directly to the ledger) skips the collector portion of P-38. The receiver portion still applies — the institution confirms the ledger or receiver stamps a non-default `SeverityNumber` per spec §4.4.4 or documents the absence in CC8.1. An institution that operates neither a collector nor a stamping receiver (chain traffic flows from SDK to ledger with no severity-handling layer) is in scope for the spec §4.4.4 rule at the SDK layer — the SDK's exported records MUST carry a non-default `SeverityNumber`, and the SOC team samples the SDK configuration in place of the receiver configuration

**P-39. Children's-data safeguards audit.**

GDPR Articles 7 and 8 grant heightened protections to children (under-16 in most EU member states; CCPA-CPRA covers California minors with a §1798.120(c) opt-out from automated decision-making for under-16s; COPPA covers under-13s in the US). When the institution's AI system makes decisions affecting minors — student-finance lending, youth banking products, parental-consent-bound account decisions — the chain captures children's data and the institution operates a heightened control posture. P-39 confirms the SOC team can mechanically test the heightened posture against the period's children's-data records. Findings are control-completeness gaps, NOT chain-integrity findings (the chain still cryptographically binds whatever was captured); the gap is in the institution's children's-data control program.

- Pull the institution's children's-data records from the chain for the period — chain entries whose `audit.*` payload identifies the data subject as a minor under the institution's classification (typical: a `audit.subject.age_class` flag the institution sets at capture, or a downstream classification the institution applies during ingest). Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample by (a) jurisdiction (EU member state where the under-16 threshold applies, US states with CCPA-CPRA-equivalent rules, US under-13 records under COPPA — sample at least 5 records per jurisdiction the institution operates in), (b) decision-class (lending decision, account-opening decision, opt-out decision — sample at least 5 records per decision-class), and (c) period segment (sample across early-period, mid-period, and late-period to detect temporal drift in capture coverage)
- For each sampled record, confirm parental consent records are on file per Article 8(1) for EU under-16s, per the analogous COPPA verifiable-parental-consent rule for US under-13s. The consent record lives in the institution's privacy-store (NOT in the chain — consent is governable personal data, the chain captures the consent-event reference). Acceptable evidence shapes: (a) a consent identifier referenced in the chain entry's `audit.consent.*` payload that resolves to a privacy-store consent record with the required attributes (consent-giver identity, relationship verification, consent date, consent scope), OR (b) the institution's privacy-store has a consent record matching the customer identifier with a date predating the chain entry's `captured_at`. Missing consent record OR a consent record dated after the decision is a control-completeness gap (severity: High; the institution made a decision affecting a minor without verifiable parental consent on file)
- Confirm tokenization is applied to behavioral inferences and children-specific data per the institution's children's-data privacy configuration. Behavioral inferences (credit-risk score from spending patterns, fraud-risk inference from transaction patterns) are tokenized for minors even when the same fields are originals for adults, per the heightened-protection posture. Children-specific data fields (school identifier, parental-relationship indicator, school-funding-program identifier) are tokenized. Acceptable evidence: the institution's CC8.1 control description names the children-specific tokenization scope, and the sampled chain entries' affected fields contain tokens (HMAC-SHA-256 shape per `privacy-by-design.md` §"Token shape") not originals. Originals where the configuration calls for tokens is a control-completeness gap
- Confirm retention timeline alignment with the institution's children's-data policy. The 7-year FFIEC default may be longer than the institution's children's-data policy supports — a child reaching adulthood within the retention window may trigger shorter retention (e.g., 4 years from the decision date, transitioning to adult retention upon majority). Pull the institution's children's-data retention policy from CC8.1; confirm the sampled records' retention disposition aligns with the policy (records past the policy's retention horizon are de-identified per `privacy-by-design.md` §"Erasure flow" — the privacy-store mapping is deleted, the chain tokens become anonymous). Records retained past the policy's horizon without de-identification is a control-completeness gap (severity: Medium when bounded and documented; High when undocumented)
- Confirm CCPA-CPRA opt-out is honored for California minors. Pull the institution's California-minor records and confirm the opt-out posture: under CCPA-CPRA §1798.120(c), the institution MUST NOT subject under-16s to automated decision-making without affirmative opt-in (parental opt-in for under-13s; minor opt-in for 13-15-year-olds). The sampled California-minor records' chain entries should reflect the opt-in evidence in the privacy-store consent record OR the absence of automated-decision chain entries for opted-out minors. A chain entry recording an automated decision on a California minor without opt-in evidence is a control-completeness gap (severity: High)
- **Failure-mode disposition.** Findings of weak parental-consent traceability, missing tokenization of behavioral inferences or children-specific data, retention beyond the children's-data policy horizon, or failure to honor CCPA-CPRA opt-out are control-completeness gaps. Severity is Medium when the gap is bounded (a single jurisdiction, a single decision-class, or a documented configuration error) and the institution's response includes restoring the control and re-running the sample. Severity is High when the gap is unbounded (the institution cannot enumerate which children's-data records are affected) OR when the gap pattern correlates with specific jurisdictions, decision-classes, or time windows in a way that suggests systematic rather than incidental loss. The findings are NOT chain-integrity findings — the chain's MAC + Merkle + HSM coverage of whatever was captured remains intact; the gap is in the institution's children's-data control program
- An institution that does not process children's data (no AI decisions affecting minors during the period) confirms the absence in CC8.1 and the SOC team verifies no `audit.subject.age_class = "minor"` records exist in the period; if any do exist, the institution's posture and its operational reality disagree and the working paper records the discrepancy for follow-up

**P-40. Privacy-by-design demonstrability audit.**

GDPR Article 25 requires privacy by design and default — and requires the institution to demonstrate the privacy-by-design implementation, not merely claim it. Demonstrability is the institution's evidence that the privacy-by-design choices were specified, built, tested, operated, and reviewed. P-40 confirms the SOC team can mechanically test the demonstrability artifacts for each chain deployment during the period. Findings are control-completeness gaps in the institution's privacy-by-design program; the chain itself is unaffected.

- Pull the institution's deployment inventory for the period — the list of `(deployment, period)` pairs covering each environment (production, staging, regional production instances, vendor-hosted deployments) where the chain operates. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample across (a) deployment environment (sample at least 3 pairs per environment the institution operates), (b) tenant (for multi-tenant deployments, sample at least 3 pairs per tenant), and (c) period segment (sample across early-period, mid-period, and late-period to detect drift in the demonstrability evidence)
- For each sampled `(deployment, period)` pair, confirm the five demonstrability artifacts are present and current per `docs/gdpr-article-25-demonstrability.md`:
  - **(1) Configuration version-control.** The institution's approved SDK privacy configuration (per `privacy-by-design.md` §"SDK configuration") is in version-controlled storage, with a documented change-management approval path. Acceptable evidence: a Git commit hash, a configuration-management-system version identifier, or an equivalent immutable reference, plus the change-management approval record (ticket, signed approval, change-advisory-board minutes). Absence of version control OR absence of change-management approval is a control-completeness gap
  - **(2) Build logs.** The institution's SDK build artifact for the deployment includes the approved configuration; the build log confirms the configuration at build time matches the version-controlled source. Acceptable evidence: a build-system log entry referencing the configuration version, OR a build-artifact manifest binding the configuration hash to the artifact. Absence of build-time configuration evidence OR a configuration mismatch between build and version-control is a control-completeness gap
  - **(3) Regression-test results.** The institution runs regression tests confirming tokenized fields produce tokens (not originals) for the sampled deployment's privacy configuration; the test results are retained for the period. Acceptable evidence: a test-system run log covering the period's deployments, with PASS results for each privacy-configuration field. Absence of regression-test evidence OR FAIL results without a documented remediation path is a control-completeness gap
  - **(4) Operation logs.** The institution logs privacy-store access (who accessed which mapping, when, why) per Article 32 audit requirements. Acceptable evidence: privacy-store access-log records covering the period for the sampled deployment, with the access-log retention matching the institution's documented retention policy. Absence of access-logging OR access-log gaps within the period is a control-completeness gap (severity: High when the gap is undocumented, since post-hoc privacy-store access cannot be reconstructed)
  - **(5) DPIA.** The institution's DPIA (per `gdpr-dpia-template.md`) covers the sampled deployment's privacy-by-design choices and the residual risk. Acceptable evidence: the DPIA document version covering the deployment's period, with the DPIA's last-review date within the institution's documented review cadence (typical: annual review, plus event-driven review on configuration changes, retention-policy changes, new-data-category additions, regulatory-guidance updates). Absence of a DPIA covering the deployment OR a DPIA last-reviewed outside the documented cadence is a control-completeness gap
- For each sampled `(deployment, period)` pair, confirm the five artifacts cross-reference consistently — the configuration version named in the build log matches the version-controlled source, the regression-test results cover the version named in the build log, the operation logs reference the deployment's tenant and configuration, and the DPIA names the configuration version covering the period. Inconsistencies between artifacts (e.g., a build log referencing configuration v3 while the DPIA covers v2) are control-completeness gaps in the institution's demonstrability program — the artifacts exist but do not compose into a coherent evidence package
- **Failure-mode disposition.** Demonstrability gaps are control-completeness findings. Severity is Medium when the gap is bounded (a single artifact missing for a single deployment, with a documented remediation path) and the institution's response includes restoring the artifact and re-running the sample. Severity is High when the gap is unbounded (the institution cannot enumerate which deployments are affected) OR when the gap pattern correlates with a specific environment, tenant, or time window in a way that suggests systematic absence rather than incidental gap. The findings are NOT chain-integrity findings — the chain's cryptographic coverage of whatever was captured remains intact; the gap is in the institution's ability to demonstrate the privacy-by-design program to a regulator, an auditor, or a customer under Article 25
- An institution lacking the five demonstrability artifacts for any sampled deployment cannot satisfy Article 25's demonstrability requirement for that deployment. The SOC team's working paper records the per-deployment artifact disposition; the institution's CC8.1 control description names the demonstrability program and the SOC team confirms the named program is operated as designed

### Round-17 G-fix procedures — FCRA reinvestigation timing and entity succession (P-56 through P-57)

The two procedures below test the institution's conformance against spec §10.11.2 (FCRA §611 reinvestigation timing) and §10.24 (entity succession), both lifted from companion documents to normative spec body in the Round-17 G-fix close-out. The procedures follow the same shape as P-1 through P-55 and integrate into the same SOC and FFIEC examination evidence repository. **P-56. FCRA §611 reinvestigation timing audit.**

Spec §10.11.2 makes FCRA §611 reinvestigation chaining a normative MUST. Each reinvestigation is a chain entry carrying the `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.*` attribute family and binding to the original adverse-action decision via `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.parent_decision_run_id` / `parent_decision_seq` per §10.11.1. The §10.11.2 schema defines REQUIRED, conditional, and RECOMMENDED attributes; the procedure tests completeness against the schema and the within-window timing against the §611 clock. Findings are control-completeness gaps, NOT chain-integrity findings (the chain still cryptographically binds whatever was captured); the gap is in the institution's FCRA dispute-trail control program. P-56 parallels P-35's translation-completeness sample — the FCRA dispute-trail is the cousin to the ECOA adverse-action translation.

- Pull a representative sample of FCRA reinvestigation chain entries (entries with any `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.*` attribute) for the period. Apply the sampling table (Sampling section). Stratify the sample by (a) outcome (sample at least 5 entries per `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.outcome` value the institution operates — `verified` | `corrected` | `deleted`), (b) extension status (sample at least 5 entries with `additional_info_received_at` present and 5 entries without — the extension distinction is load-bearing for the within-window check), (c) furnisher path (sample at least 5 entries where `furnisher_notified_at` is present and 5 where it is absent — the institution's role as CRA, furnisher, or both determines applicability), and (d) period segment (early / mid / late period to detect temporal drift)
- For each sampled reinvestigation entry, confirm the REQUIRED attributes per spec §10.11.2 are present and well-formed: `dispute_received_at` (RFC 3339 UTC), `reinvestigation_completed_at` (RFC 3339 UTC), `consumer_notified_at` (RFC 3339 UTC), `outcome` (enum — `verified` | `corrected` | `deleted`), `parent_decision_run_id` (string), `parent_decision_seq` (integer). Missing any REQUIRED attribute is a control-completeness gap
- For each sampled entry, compute the within-window check: `(reinvestigation_completed_at − dispute_received_at)` MUST NOT exceed 30 days, OR 45 days when `additional_info_received_at` is present (the §611(a)(3) extension). A sampled entry exceeding the applicable window is a substantive FCRA finding (severity: High; the institution missed the §611 reinvestigation clock) that the SOC firm and the FFIEC examiner both consume as a regulatory-compliance gap
- For sampled entries where `furnisher_notified_at` is present, compute `(furnisher_notified_at − dispute_received_at)`. The §611(a)(2) 5-business-day notification expectation is the conformance bar; entries exceeding 5 business days from dispute receipt to furnisher notification is a CRA-side §611 finding. For entries where the institution is the furnisher (receiving notice from a CRA), confirm `furnisher_notified_at` is present and dated before `reinvestigation_completed_at` — a furnisher reinvestigation completed before the institution received notice is a logical inconsistency surfaced through P-6 anomaly review
- For each sampled entry, confirm `parent_decision_run_id` and `parent_decision_seq` link to a chained adverse-action decision under §10.11.1 (the underlying entry whose `audit.ecoa.adverse_action.*` reasons schema was the consumer's basis for dispute). The verifier walks the parent linkage per spec §4.4; the SOC team confirms the linkage resolves to a decision entry whose payload reflects the institution's FCRA-relevant decision class (typically a credit-reporting decision or a furnisher-side data-furnishing event). A reinvestigation entry with no parent linkage, OR with a parent linkage that does not resolve to an FCRA-relevant decision, is a control-completeness gap
- Cross-reference `docs/customer-dispute-procedures.md` for the FCRA-side dispute disposition path. The procedure's purpose is to confirm that a CFPB / FCRA examiner asking "did this consumer's §611 dispute close within the regulatory window?" can answer from the chain entries alone, without the institution surfacing supplementary evidence per spec §10.11.2
- **Failure-mode disposition.** Reinvestigation-completeness gaps are control-completeness findings (severity: Medium when the gap rate is below the institution's documented threshold for routine variance; severity: High when the gap rate exceeds the threshold OR when the gap pattern correlates with specific outcomes, furnisher paths, or time windows in a way that suggests systematic rather than incidental loss). Within-window violations (the §611 30/45-day clock missed) are substantive FCRA findings independent of the control-completeness disposition — the chain captures the violation; the institution's compliance program responds to the regulatory consequence
- **Institutions not subject to FCRA §611.** Institutions whose operations do not touch FCRA-regulated consumer reporting (no CRA function, no furnisher function) confirm the absence in CC8.1 and the SOC team verifies no `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.*` entries exist in the period; if any do exist, the institution's posture and its operational reality disagree and the working paper records the discrepancy for follow-up

Cross-reference: spec §10.11.2; spec §10.11 (ECOA cousin clock); spec §10.11.1 (the adverse-action reasons schema this entry chains back to); FCRA §611 (15 USC §1681i); P-35 (the ECOA translation-completeness procedure this one parallels); `docs/customer-dispute-procedures.md` (consumer-facing dispute disposition); `docs/regulator-pack/cfpb-overlay.md` (Bureau-side examiner orientation).

**P-57. Entity succession audit.**

Spec §10.24 makes entity-succession events (mergers, acquisitions, divestitures, renames, subsidiary transfers) a normative MUST. Each succession is recorded as a `chain.entity_succession` operational event under §10.2 carrying dual signatures from authorized signers of both the from-entity and the to-entity, bound under the seal of the transfer-day per §4.3 sign_payload v1.0b. The procedure tests completeness against the §10.24 schema and continuity against the chain-keying discipline. Findings are control-completeness gaps in the institution's M&A handoff program — the chain itself remains intact across the boundary; the gap is in the institution's evidence that the succession was governed by §10.24's discipline.

- Confirm the institution operated a succession event during the period. The sources are (a) the institution's CC8.1 control description naming any merger, acquisition, divestiture, rename, or subsidiary transfer that affected chain operation during the period, (b) the institution's regulator-filing record for any FDIC, OCC, or FRB filing during the period that would imply a succession event, and (c) the chain itself — the operational events list MUST contain a `chain.entity_succession` event for each succession the CC8.1 names. An institution operating no successions during the period confirms the absence in CC8.1; the SOC team verifies no `chain.entity_succession` events exist in the period; if any do exist, the institution's posture and its operational reality disagree and the working paper records the discrepancy for follow-up
- For each `chain.entity_succession` event during the period, confirm the REQUIRED attributes per spec §10.24 are present and well-formed: `from_entity_legal_name` (string), `to_entity_legal_name` (string), `effective_utc` (RFC 3339 UTC), `kind` (enum — `merger` | `acquisition` | `divestiture` | `rename` | `subsidiary_transfer`), `dual_signatures` array (exactly two signature objects per §10.17 schema). Missing any REQUIRED attribute is a control-completeness gap
- For each event, confirm `dual_signatures` contains exactly two signature objects. The first is the from-entity's authorized signer; the second is the to-entity's authorized signer. Each signature object MUST carry `role`, `name`, and `entity_affiliation` per §10.17; the `entity_affiliation` field is the discriminator between the from-entity and the to-entity signers under M&A scenarios where the same individual may sign for different entities at the boundary. A succession event with fewer or more than two signature objects, OR with two signature objects whose `entity_affiliation` values are identical, is a control-completeness gap
- For each event, confirm the seal of the transfer-day (the seal whose `seal_date` covers `effective_utc`) covers the succession event under §4.3 sign_payload v1.0b. The verifier's PASS over the transfer-day seal IS the integrity-bound record that the succession event was bound under the to-entity's signature on the transfer-day. A succession event present in the operational events list but not covered by a verified seal is a control-completeness gap (the event was emitted but the seal-time integrity binding did not complete)
- For events recording a `tenant_id` rename (when `from_tenant_id` and `to_tenant_id` are both present), confirm the chain entries before `effective_utc` are under `from_tenant_id` and entries after are under `to_tenant_id`. The verifier walks both chains independently using the per-`tenant_id` HKDF binding (§4.1); both chains MUST verify successfully across the boundary. A tenant_id rename where post-succession entries continue under the old `tenant_id` (or pre-succession entries appear under the new `tenant_id`) is a control-completeness gap that may indicate misconfigured SDK binding at the succession boundary
- For events with `regulator_filing_id` present, confirm the filing identifier resolves against the regulator's filing record. The SOC team's working paper records the filing-record reference; the institution's compliance team provides the resolution. A `regulator_filing_id` value that does not resolve against the regulator's record is a control-completeness gap (the institution recorded a filing identifier the regulator does not recognize) routed to the institution's CC8.1 succession procedure
- Confirm the institution's `chain.coverage_map_published` event under §10.19 (Round-17 M&A-P3) re-emits across the succession boundary. The post-succession coverage map is anchored on the to-entity's chain so an acquirer-side auditor running an 18-month lookback determines which map version was in force on each side of the succession. A succession without a corresponding `chain.coverage_map_published` event covering the post-succession period is a control-completeness gap in the institution's coverage-map versioning program
- **Failure-mode disposition.** Succession-completeness gaps are control-completeness findings (severity: Medium when the gap is bounded — a single succession during the period with a documented remediation path; severity: High when the institution operated multiple successions with systematic gaps OR when the gap pattern indicates the succession was operated outside §10.24's discipline entirely). The findings are NOT chain-integrity findings — the chain's MAC + Merkle + HSM coverage of whatever was captured remains intact across the boundary; the gap is in the institution's evidence that the succession was governed by the binding spec section rather than only by the informative supplement

Cross-reference: spec §10.24; spec §10.17 (the signatory schema `dual_signatures` follows including the `entity_affiliation` discriminator field); spec §10.18 (CC8.1 cross-referencing — the institution's CC8.1 cross-references §10.24 for the succession procedure); spec §10.19 (the chain-coverage map re-emission across the succession boundary); spec §4.3 sign_payload v1.0b (the dual signatures bind under the v1.0b seal); `docs/m-and-a-handoff.md` (operational supplement promoted to normative-supplement under Round-17 M&A-G1).

## Testing approach

For each procedure:

1. **Identify the control claim.** What specifically is the institution claiming?
2. **Identify the evidence.** What records, logs, or system outputs support the claim?
3. **Identify the test.** What testing procedure would verify the claim?
4. **Execute the test.** Pull the evidence; perform the testing.
5. **Document the result.** Pass / pass with note / fail.
6. **Confirm the institution's understanding.** Discuss anomalous results with the institution.
7. **Document the disposition.** Accepted / accepted with note / not accepted.

## Working-paper preservation

Evidence and test results are retained in the SOC firm's standard working-paper system. Chain-specific evidence (verifier output, operational logs, ledger snapshots) may be voluminous; the SOC firm retains representative samples and references to the institution-side full evidence.

## Coordination with FFIEC examination

When the institution is subject to both SOC engagement and FFIEC examination:

- The SOC firm and the FFIEC examiner consume the same evidence base
- The SOC firm typically tests during the reporting period
- The FFIEC examiner typically tests during the examination cycle
- Both produce findings; both are inputs to the institution's broader assurance picture

The chain provides the consistent evidence; SOC and FFIEC interpretations are independent.

### Evidence sequencing between SOC and FFIEC

Both consumers read from the same evidence base — operational events (spec §10.2), verifier output, the IKM roster, control-evidence-events, the daily seal records, and the institution's anomaly-evaluation records per `docs/anomaly-documentation-template.md`. The SOC engagement tests the operational controls during the reporting period and produces an opinion on control-design and operating-effectiveness. The FFIEC examination tests current-state controls at the examiner's cycle and produces a regulatory finding letter. The chain itself is the same artifact for both; the audience and the question being asked differ.

**What the SOC engagement produces vs what the FFIEC examination consumes.** The SOC report typically issues 60–90 days after period-end with the SOC firm's opinion, the description of controls (the institution's CC1 through CC9 narrative), the test procedures executed (the P-N procedures from this doc that applied to the engagement), and the test results. The FFIEC examiner consumes the SOC report at examination start as a baseline assurance artifact — the examiner reads the SOC opinion, the scope, the period covered, and the findings, then forms a view on which areas need direct examiner re-testing vs which areas the SOC report's coverage is sufficient. The institution provides the SOC report to the examiner as part of the standard examination request list.

**How the SOC report's findings inform the FFIEC examination.** A clean SOC report (unqualified opinion, no exceptions) typically narrows the examiner's direct re-testing to current-state evidence — the examiner samples recent verifier output, recent reconciliation events, recent seal records, and confirms the controls the SOC tested still operate as described. A qualified SOC report (exceptions noted) typically expands the examiner's re-testing in the affected control areas; the examiner re-tests the controls the SOC found exceptions on, plus the institution's remediation evidence for those exceptions. The relationship is asymmetric — a clean SOC narrows examination scope, exceptions expand it. The institution that ships a qualified SOC report should expect a deeper examination cycle in the affected areas.

**Whether the institution shares SOC working papers with the FFIEC examiner.** The SOC report itself is shared. The SOC firm's underlying working papers (the test-execution evidence, the engagement memos, the reviewer notes) are the SOC firm's property under professional standards and are NOT routinely shared with the FFIEC examiner; the SOC firm's contractual confidentiality and the AICPA's working-paper retention rules govern that disposition. When the FFIEC examiner needs deeper evidence than the SOC report exposes, the examiner requests the underlying institution-side evidence directly (the verifier output, the operational events, the change-management records the SOC firm relied on) — not the SOC firm's working papers. The institution operates as the evidence custodian for both consumers; the SOC firm's working papers are an internal SOC-firm artifact.

**Timing dependencies.** The SOC report typically issues 60–90 days after period-end. The FFIEC examination expects current-state evidence at examination start, which may be 6–12 months after the SOC report's period-end (community banks examined on 18-month cycles often have a 6–9-month gap between SOC report period-end and the next examination). The institution MUST be prepared to provide both (a) the SOC report covering the historical period, AND (b) current-state evidence covering the period from SOC report period-end through examination start. Current-state evidence is the same evidence shape as the period evidence — verifier output, seal records, operational events, reconciliation events — but covering the post-SOC interval. The institution that maintains continuous evidence-production during the post-SOC interval has a low examination-prep burden; the institution that treats the SOC engagement as a periodic event and lets evidence-production lapse afterward faces examination-prep crunch.

**How the institution avoids duplicating evidence-production work.** The chain is designed so one body of evidence serves both consumers. The institution SHOULD operate evidence-production as a continuous discipline (the operational events, the verifier runs, the reconciliation cadence, the seal records emit on their natural cadence regardless of audit cycle), and the institution SHOULD maintain a single evidence-index that both the SOC firm and the FFIEC examiner consume. Typical posture: a control-evidence repository organized by P-N procedure that catalogs the evidence artifact, the period covered, the storage location, and the retention disposition. The SOC firm and the FFIEC examiner each pull from the same index; the institution does not produce two evidence packages. Evidence-production work is duplicated only when the institution operates separate evidence pipelines for SOC vs FFIEC, which is a control-program-maturity finding rather than a chain-conformance finding — the chain itself produces a single evidence stream; duplication arises from institutional process, not from the spec.

**Cross-references between SOC working papers and FFIEC working papers.** The SOC firm's working papers reference the institution's evidence by storage location and date. The FFIEC examiner's working papers reference the same evidence by the same storage locations. The institution's evidence-index is the bridge — both sets of working papers cite the institution-side artifact, not each other. When the FFIEC examiner explicitly relies on a SOC test result (the examiner reads the SOC report, decides the SOC's coverage is sufficient for a given control area, and skips direct re-testing), the examiner's working papers cite the SOC report by section and the SOC test procedure by P-N reference. The SOC firm's working papers do not cross-reference the FFIEC examination — the SOC engagement is independent of any future examination. The institution that maintains stable P-N references across SOC and FFIEC work products makes the cross-citation unambiguous; renaming or renumbering procedures between engagements creates traceability friction that compounds across audit cycles.
