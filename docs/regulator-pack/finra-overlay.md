# FINRA (SRO) Articulation Overlay

> **What this doc is.** An articulation overlay mapping the FFIEC chain-of-custody specification (PRD-3 / 0.3.0) onto FINRA's supervisory framework for member broker-dealers. FINRA is a **self-regulatory organization (SRO)**, not a government agency; its members are SEC-registered broker-dealers, and FINRA Rule 4511 incorporates the SEA 17a-3 / 17a-4 recordkeeping regime by reference. This overlay composes on top of `sec-overlay.md` — read that first for the §240.17a-4(f) recordkeeping foundation.

> **What this doc is NOT.** Not a normative extension. Not a substitute for the member's WSPs (written supervisory procedures) or compliance program. Spec §1.2 (epistemic scope) governs: the chain proves what was captured and that it was not tampered with after capture; it does not prove substantive suitability, best-interest, or fair-and-balanced content compliance.

> **A note on accuracy discipline.** This overlay separates two things and never conflates them: **[SETTLED RULE]** — rule text in force today (Rules 4511, 3110, 2210; the SEA 17a-4(f) regime), and **[EXAM SIGNAL]** — the 2026 FINRA Annual Regulatory Oversight Report's GenAI expectations, which are stated exam expectations and observed practices, **not rule text**. There is no FINRA AI-specific rule as of mid-2026, and none is formally proposed.

## 2. Rule 4511 → SEA 17a-4(f) recordkeeping [SETTLED RULE]

FINRA Rule 4511 requires members to preserve books and records per the SEA 17a-3 / 17a-4 regime. The recordkeeping mapping is therefore **identical to the SEC overlay's §2** — spec **§5.2.3** maps the §240.17a-4(f) audit-trail alternative element-by-element onto chain primitives, and a verifier `Status: PASS` is the audit-trail-alternative conformance demonstration. Nothing FINRA-specific changes the mapping; Rule 4511 is the incorporation vehicle, and the settled 17a-4(f) audit-trail alternative (not the WORM path) is the doctrinally-correct fit for a tamper-evident chain.

**[OPEN QUESTION]** Whether AI prompts/outputs are themselves "records … as such" under 17a-4(b)(4) is unsettled (FINRA RN 25-07, April 2025, requested comment; not answered as of mid-2026). This overlay does not resolve it.

## 3. Rule 3110 supervision [SETTLED RULE]

Rule 3110 requires a member to supervise its business activities; the member remains responsible for outputs "regardless of whether generated by a human or by AI technology." The chain supplies the supervisory-review evidence:

| Rule 3110 element | Chain-of-custody evidence |
|---|---|
| Human-in-the-loop review of AI-assisted activity | §10.50 output-grounding review (signed reviewer, `audit.review.role`, outcome) |
| Actor / who acted under whose authority | §14.6 `audit.actor.*` (authenticated user hash, method, delegation chain) |
| Supervisory ownership map | §10.80 three-lines-of-defense ownership map |
| Exceptions / anomalies surfaced for review | §10.81 anomaly taxonomy (CC7.2 cross-walk) |

The chain proves the supervisory events occurred and were integrity-bound; whether the supervision was *reasonable* under Rule 3110 is the member's substantive evidence.

## 4. Rule 2210 communications — principal pre-approval [SETTLED RULE]

Rule 2210 classifies communications by audience and, for **retail communications distributed to more than 25 retail investors within any 30-calendar-day period**, requires a registered principal to approve the communication **before first use** (pre-approval). Spec **§10.84 (communication principal-preapproval ordering)** is the chain-side discipline that proves the *ordering* — approval preceded the send — not merely that both events occurred.

| Rule 2210 element | Chain-of-custody evidence |
|---|---|
| Audience classification | `audit.communication.audience` (`retail` \| `institutional` \| `correspondence` \| institution-named) on the communication event |
| Registered-principal approval | §10.50 review event with `audit.review.role = "registered_principal"`, parent-linked to the communication |
| The send | §14.8 `audit.downstream_action.*` with `action_kind ∈ {communication_sent, notification_sent}`, parent-linked |
| Pre-approval ordering | The verifier checks `approval.signed_at_utc ≤ send.applied_at_utc` and emits the marker `communication_principal_preapproval_verified` on success |

**Soft-enforcement (important).** §10.84 is a completeness check, not a chain-integrity gate. An ordering violation surfaces as an anomaly line under `Status: PASS` (`communication principal-preapproval ordering: approval did not precede send at seq N`), and a retail communication with a send but no bound principal approval surfaces as `… no registered-principal approval bound at seq N`. The chain stays `PASS`; the anomaly is what a FINRA examiner or SOC engagement reviews. The verifier reads only the ordering it can prove from the bound timestamps — whether the communication was correctly classified `retail`, and whether the >25-in-30-days threshold applied, are member-side determinations the WSPs name and the SOC engagement tests. `institutional` and `correspondence` communications are outside the pre-approval ordering check.

Conformance vectors `090-communication-preapproval-ordering-pass` (approval precedes send → marker) and `091-communication-preapproval-ordering-anomaly` (approval after send → anomaly) pin the behavior.

## 5. Reg BI [SETTLED RULE]

Reg BI applies to FINRA members exactly as in the SEC overlay's §3 — an AI-generated recommendation to a retail customer is still a recommendation. The recommendation-with-lineage composition (§10.47 generation + §14.7 substrate + §10.50 review + §14.8 downstream action + §4.4 parent-linkage) is the evidence; substantive best-interest compliance is member-side.

## 6. 2026 FINRA report GenAI expectations [EXAM SIGNAL — NOT RULE TEXT]

The 2026 FINRA Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (published Dec 2025) has a dedicated "GenAI: Continuing and Emerging Trends" section stating FINRA's current exam expectations. These are **observed practices and stated expectations an examiner may ask about**, grounded in the existing rules above — **not** a new AI rule. Every item below maps to a chain capability that already ships:

| 2026-report expectation [EXAM SIGNAL] | Chain capability (already shipped) |
|---|---|
| "Storing prompt and output logs for accountability" | §10.47 four-tuple binds `system_prompt_sha256` / `user_prompt_sha256` / `output_sha256` (the chain binds hashes; the logs themselves live in the member's 17a-4-governed store) |
| "Tracking which model version was used and when" | §10.47 `model_id` + `inference_at_utc` (required); §10.48 `model_version` + `model_weight_hash` |
| "Validation and human-in-the-loop review" | §10.50 output-grounding review (signed, role-tagged) |
| "Track and log AI agent actions and decisions" | §14.6 `audit.actor.*` (+ delegation chain for on-behalf-of/agent) + §14.8 `audit.downstream_action.*` + §14.7 substrate |
| Guardrails / restricted agent behavior | Member-side control; the chain integrity-binds the actions the guardrails permit or block |

The mapping is a convenience for an examiner following the 2026 report; it does not elevate the report's language to an obligation.

## 7. Bottom line for the FINRA examiner

1. **Recordkeeping (Rule 4511 → 17a-4(f))** — the chain satisfies the settled audit-trail alternative (spec §5.2.3), demonstrable by running the verifier. SRO status changes nothing: the verifier's principal is whoever holds the seal-signing public key, and a FINRA examiner holds it exactly as a government examiner does.
2. **Supervision (Rule 3110)** — HITL review, actor identity, and the three-lines map are integrity-bound.
3. **Communications (Rule 2210)** — §10.84 proves registered-principal pre-approval preceded the send for retail communications (soft-enforcement anomaly when it did not).
4. **Reg BI** — recommendation lineage is integrity-bound and linked.
5. **2026-report GenAI expectations [EXAM SIGNAL]** — every stated expectation maps to a shipped chain capability; none is a rule.

What the chain does NOT deliver: substantive suitability/best-interest/fair-and-balanced compliance, model accuracy, recipient counting for the >25-in-30-days threshold, or an answer to the RN 25-07 "records as such" question. Those are member-side and unsettled-law matters.
