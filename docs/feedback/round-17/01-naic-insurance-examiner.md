# Round 17 Outside Reviewer — NAIC State Insurance Examiner

**Reviewer persona:** State insurance department market-conduct examiner, fifteen years on a Northeast DOI, last six on a state team that examines AI/ML in personal-lines underwriting, claims triage, and pricing. Familiar with the NAIC AI Model Bulletin (December 2023), Colorado Reg 10-1-1 (SB21-169), NYDFS Insurance Circular Letter No. 7 (2024), and the model-governance and disparate-impact-testing expectations my colleagues bring to a Section 504 / Section 308 review.
**Spec version reviewed:** v1.0a (chain-of-custody-v1.md, 1358 lines)
**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Question budget used:** 6 of 8

## Overall verdict

I would conditionally sign off on a carrier using this spec as the integrity substrate for AI-decisioning evidence — but only on the integrity question. The spec is a clean, well-grounded chain-of-custody primitive set; what it proves (per §1.2 (a)–(b): "what the AI said at a specific time" and "the record was not tampered with after capture") is exactly the foundation a state DOI subpoena demands, and the §7 byte-exact verifier procedure plus FIPS-validated HSM custody (§10.5) gives me a defensible reproduction path. What the spec explicitly does NOT prove (§1.2 (c)–(e): factual accuracy, policy compliance, freedom from bias) is precisely the territory my market-conduct exam lives in. The spec authors are honest about that — they call it "the integrity foundation, not the truth foundation" — and I respect the discipline. The gap, from a state-DOI lens, is that several insurance-specific evidentiary regimes (disparate-impact testing artifacts, protected-class proxy disclosure, rate-filing actuarial-memo linkage, champion/challenger model swap recording) do not have a normative attribute family the way ECOA adverse-action (§10.11) or routing classifier output (§4.4.1 `audit.routing.classifier_output`) do. A carrier deploying this spec would still hand me a coherent integrity record; they would still need to assemble the disparate-impact and rate-filing evidence on the side, in formats that vary carrier-by-carrier.

## Findings

### Gaps (something a state DOI would flat-out reject)

**None observed at the integrity level.** The spec answers test questions 1, 2, 3, and 5 cleanly — see Strengths. There are no gaps that would cause me to refuse the chain itself.

### Partials (something present but insufficient)

**P-1. Protected-class proxy attributes are not a first-class chain concept.** The spec captures whatever the institution emits under the `audit.*` namespace and integrity-binds it (§5 canonical-form coverage), and §4.4.1 normates the `audit.routing.classifier_output` family with `classifier_input_hash`, per-class `classifier_scores`, and `classifier_decision`. That gets me partway — a language-detection or intent-classification step that drives an underwriting-route decision IS chain-bound. What is missing is a normative attribute family for **input-feature recording on the underwriting decision itself** — the per-decision feature vector (or hash + reference to a feature-store version) that lets me ask "did ZIP code, occupation code, vehicle make-model-year, or credit-tier-band drive this decline?" Today I have to take the carrier's `gen_ai_parameters` schema (§4.4 attribute, "the institution determines the schema") on faith. For NAIC AI Model Bulletin Section 4 ("documentation of data used in AI Systems") and Colorado Reg 10-1-1 quantitative-testing requirements, I want a normative `audit.underwriting.features.*` family analogous to `audit.routing.classifier_*`.

**P-2. Disparate-impact testing artifacts are not part of the chain-coverage map.** §10.19 ("Chain-coverage boundary documentation") names categories like "external evidentiary artifacts hash-anchored" and §10.21 names `fairness_audit_report_sha256` for cross-vendor model handover. Both are conformant for what they cover. They do NOT name the **per-period disparate-impact / adverse-impact-ratio test results** my market-conduct exam looks for under NAIC AI Model Bulletin Section 4.3 and Colorado Reg 10-1-1 §6. A carrier running the four-fifths-rule test quarterly should be able to chain-anchor the test report (input population hash, protected-class proxy methodology, AIR result by class, remediation disposition) under a normative `audit.disparate_impact.*` family. Today the spec lets them anchor it as `audit.external_artifact.*` (§10.19), which works mechanically but does not signal to a fellow examiner that this is THE quarterly DI test and not, say, a customs broker case snapshot.

**P-3. Champion/challenger swap is captured as `audit.deployment.intent = ab_test` but the rate-filing linkage is missing.** §4.4.2 deployment-intent capture answers test question 2 well — the chain shows which model version produced each decision (`gen_ai.response.model` is REQUIRED per §4.4) and the deployment-intent enum disambiguates A/B test from production from canary from vendor-reroute. What is NOT in the schema is a back-reference to the carrier's **rate-filing form-and-rate identifier** (the SERFF tracking number, the state-approved actuarial memorandum version) under which the model is permitted to operate. If a carrier promotes a challenger to production without an updated rate filing, my market-conduct review should be able to read that mismatch from the chain. Today I would have to cross-reference outside the chain: take `gen_ai.response.model` from the chain and ask the carrier for the SERFF mapping in a separate data call.

**P-4. Cross-border transfer family (§4.4.1 `audit.cross_border_transfer.*`) is "advisory at v1.0a" — for state insurance privacy law it should be required when applicable.** Test question 5: a carrier whose parent is in another country (a Bermuda-domiciled holding company, a German parent, a Japanese reinsurer in the chain) MUST under several state insurance privacy laws (NY DFS Part 500, Cal Insurance Code §791) document each cross-border transfer of consumer information for an underwriting or claims-triage decision. The advisory posture is fine for jurisdictions where the chain is one of several evidence regimes; for state insurance privacy the regulator-side expectation is that the lawful-basis attribute set is on the chain entry the moment the transfer occurs. Closing this would mean elevating §4.4.1's `audit.cross_border_transfer.*` from "informative attribute, advisory under privacy regulators" to "REQUIRED when the chain entry corresponds to a transfer subject to state insurance privacy law named in the institution's CC8.1." The mechanical change is small; the conformance-bar shift is what matters.

### Nits (cosmetic / wording / cross-reference)

**N-1.** §13 ("Stakeholder navigation") names FFIEC IT Examiner, FFIEC Cybersecurity Specialist, MRM committee, SOC engagement team, and several others. There is no entry for "State insurance department market-conduct examiner" or "State DOI rate-and-form examiner." Adding one (with cross-references to §4.4.2 deployment-intent, §10.21 model-handover, §4.4.1 routing classifier output, §10.11 ECOA-adjacent translation discipline) would let a first-time DOI reader land in the spec faster. I came in through §11 References and §13 to find my stakeholder slice and there wasn't one.

**N-2.** §10.11 names ECOA adverse-action notice translation as a MUST. Several states (NY, CA, CO) require the same translation discipline for **insurance adverse-action notices** (declination, non-renewal, rate-up tier assignment). The §10.11 schema is conformant for those use cases by analogy, but the section name is `ECOA adverse-action notice translation` and the section text references `customer-dispute-procedures.md §"Adverse-action notices (ECOA)"` only. A one-line cross-reference to "and analogous state-insurance-law adverse-action notices" would let a state DOI examiner confirm the schema applies without filing a clarification request.

**N-3.** §4.4.2 deployment-intent enum lists `production | ab_test | canary | multi_region_drift | vendor_reroute_observed | unknown`. For insurance specifically, two more values would be load-bearing: `regulatory_sandbox` (state DOI sandbox programs explicitly allow models that are not yet in an approved rate filing) and `disparate_impact_test_run` (a model invocation made for the carrier's own quarterly DI testing rather than for a real applicant). Without these, both end up bucketed under `production` or `ab_test` and the per-decision-count distribution analysis (§4.4.2 MRM dispositions table) cannot disambiguate them.

## Strengths

- **§4.4.2 deployment-intent capture answers test question 2 cleanly.** Champion/challenger swaps appear in the chain with `audit.deployment.intent = ab_test` plus `gen_ai.response.model` (REQUIRED per §4.4 — "vendors silently re-route between model versions during outages; the response-side model identifier is the load-bearing record"). I can pull every decision under each model version for any time window without trusting the carrier's narrative. The MRM disposition table (§4.4.2) names `vendor_reroute_observed` as a control-completeness signal — exactly the discipline I need for the silent-vendor-substitution case my colleagues warn each other about.

- **§10.21 cross-vendor model-handover schema answers test question 3.** A carrier using a third-party scoring vendor (LexisNexis, TransUnion DriverRisk, Verisk) chain-anchors `audit.model_handover.provider`, `model_artifact_sha256`, `model_card_sha256`, `fairness_audit_report_sha256`, and `provider_chain_entry_id`. Bidirectional verification with the vendor's chain (§10.21 "Cross-anchor verification") is exactly what I want when I'm trying to confirm what the vendor delivered matches what the carrier deployed.

- **§7 byte-exact verifier procedure plus §10.12 exit-code contract answers test question 1.** I can subpoena a single underwriting decision from two years ago, run a witness-mode verifier (§7 "Witness-verifier mode") against the carrier's audit file without holding the IKM, and get a structurally-bound PASS-or-FAIL on the byte content. With cooperation and the IKM-held-in-escrow path, I get the full key-bound PASS. The Daubert four-factor grounding (§1.1) reads like it was written by someone who has been on a witness stand.

- **§10.20 training-data retention floor (deployment-window-bounded plus 60–90 day investigation buffer) closes a gap I see at almost every carrier I examine.** Carriers regularly destroy training data on a privacy-minimization clock that runs out before the model is decommissioned, and when a regression appears six months in there is no path back to root cause. The §10.20 normative MUST is the right shape.

- **§1.2 epistemic-scope discipline.** "The chain proves what the AI said at time T; that is not the same as the AI was right, complied, or unbiased." That paragraph is doing more work than the authors may realize — it pre-empts the carrier's instinct to overclaim what an integrity chain proves, and it leaves the disparate-impact and rate-compliance evidence regimes where they belong: as separate, parallel, carrier-side responsibilities the chain composes alongside.

## Questions to the spec authors

1. **§4.4 `gen_ai_parameters` schema is institution-determined.** Have you considered an `audit.underwriting.features.*` (or `audit.decision.features.*`) normative family with `feature_vector_hash`, `feature_store_version`, and per-feature inclusion flags so a state DOI examiner can read protected-class-proxy attribution mechanically rather than parsing the institution's free-form schema? If so, what is the v1.x roadmap?

2. **§10.19 chain-coverage map names "external evidentiary artifacts hash-anchored."** Is there a candidate v1.x normative `audit.disparate_impact.*` family (per-period AIR by protected class, four-fifths-rule disposition, remediation reference) that would land disparate-impact test results on the chain in a regulator-readable shape rather than under the generic external-artifact bucket?

3. **§4.4.2 deployment-intent enum.** Is there an extension path for `regulatory_sandbox` and `disparate_impact_test_run` enum values that would not break the closed-set discipline §3 names for `chain_kind`?

4. **§4.4.1 `audit.cross_border_transfer.*` is informative.** Under what circumstances would the working group consider elevating it to "REQUIRED when the chain entry corresponds to a transfer subject to a regulator-named privacy regime"? State insurance privacy laws (NY, CA, CO, MA) are the immediate driver from my lens.

5. **§10.21 model-handover schema lists `fairness_audit_report_sha256` as "when applicable" with EU AI Act Article 11/12 as the trigger.** For US state insurance, the trigger is NAIC AI Model Bulletin Section 4.3 plus state-specific quantitative-testing rules (Colorado Reg 10-1-1, NYDFS Circular Letter 7). Would the working group consider naming "or US state-insurance disparate-impact-testing regime named in the institution's CC8.1" as an additional trigger?

6. **§10.11 ECOA adverse-action notice translation.** Would the working group consider renaming this section to "Adverse-action notice translation (ECOA and state-insurance analog)" or adding a §10.11.1 sub-section for state-insurance-law adverse-action notices? The schema is already conformant by analogy; the naming is what would let a state DOI examiner cite the section directly in an examination report.
