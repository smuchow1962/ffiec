# Round 17 Outside Reviewer — CFPB Consumer-Finance Examiner

**Reviewer persona:** Twelve years at the CFPB Office of Supervision Examinations, focused on fintech and bank consumer-credit operations. I routinely issue Civil Investigative Demands, examine adverse-action notice generation, audit dispute-resolution flows under Regulation B (ECOA), Regulation V (FCRA), and the Bureau's §1031/1036 UDAAP authority. My read on any logging substrate is "can the consumer get the data the law promises them, can the bank prove what its model actually weighed, and is the dispute trail itself audit-grade."
**Spec version reviewed:** v1.0a (chain-of-custody-v1.md, 1358 lines)
**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Question budget used:** 6 of 8

## Overall verdict

I would accept this spec as a defensible substrate for adverse-action evidence and consumer-dispute reproduction. It is more careful than most of what I see in industry submissions: §10.11 makes the ECOA translation step a chained event with a parent linkage to the underlying model call, §5.2 sets out a clean best-evidence posture (captured JSON for content, canonical bytes for integrity), and the customer-dispute-procedures.md companion lays out a witness-mode verifier path that lets a consumer's expert verify integrity without ever holding the institution's IKM. That last property matters — most of what I examine fails this test, because "tamper-evident" reduces to "trust the bank's self-attestation." Here it doesn't.

What's missing for full Bureau use is on the FCRA side and on the class-of-consumers retrieval side. The spec is built around `(tenant_id, run_id, seq)` and explicitly says the chain "is keyed by `(tenant_id, run_id, seq)`, not by consumer identity" — which is correct for integrity but leaves the Bureau dependent on the institution's customer-correlation index (CUEC) for any consumer-keyed retrieval, including the kind a CID asks for. The spec does not normate that index. It also does not address FCRA §611 reinvestigation timing anywhere I can find. The ECOA 30-day clock is named explicitly; the FCRA 30/45-day clock is not.

## Findings

### Gaps (something CFPB would not accept)

**FCRA §611 reinvestigation timing is absent.** Search of the spec body returns zero hits for `FCRA`, `§611`, `1681i`, `reinvestigation`. §10.11 normates ECOA adverse-action translation and customer-dispute-procedures.md §"Adverse-action notices (ECOA)" anchors the 30-day ECOA clock against `entry 1's captured_at` and `entry 2's delivery_timestamp`. There is no analogous chained event family for FCRA dispute reinvestigation — no `audit.fcra.reinvestigation.*` schema with `dispute_received_at`, `additional_info_received_at` (which extends the clock to 45 days under §611(a)(3)), `furnisher_notified_at`, `reinvestigation_completed_at`, or `consumer_notified_at`. For a bank using AI in a credit-reporting capacity (and many of our supervised entities do), the dispute trail is unchained. Closing this is a §10.11-shaped addition: a normative `audit.fcra.*` event family with the timing anchors the §611 clock turns on, plus an audit procedure paralleling P-35.

**Consumer-keyed retrieval (CID-class production) has no normative form.** A Bureau CID typically reads "produce all adverse-action decisions for consumers in [ZIP, demographic class, product line] during [period]." The spec's only addressing scheme is `(tenant_id, run_id, seq)`. customer-dispute-procedures.md §"CFPB-specific procedures" punts to "the bank's chain-ops team produces the affected runs" via "the bank's customer-correlation-index (CUEC)." That index is institution-internal and the spec does not normate its shape, integrity, or reproducibility. A CID response built off an unchained, institution-controlled index is exactly the kind of asymmetric-evidence move §10.11 closed for the translation step. Either the index entries themselves should be chain entries (the recommended posture, by analogy to §10.11), or the spec should normate a CUEC integrity attestation that the Bureau's verifier can independently check.

### Partials (something present but insufficient)

**ECOA "specific reasons" requirement is one inferential step away from being directly answerable.** Reg B §1002.9(a)(2)(i) requires the bank's notice to disclose the "principal reason(s) for the adverse action" — and on a model-driven decision, the Bureau's question is whether those listed reasons match what the model actually weighted. customer-dispute-procedures.md shows `audit.ecoa.adverse_action.reasons = "[institution's structured reasons payload]"` in its worked example, but that schema is not normated anywhere — the worked example treats it as institution-defined. The translation step has a normative attribute schema (§10.11, eight attributes); the underlying adverse-action reasons step does not. To answer "do the listed reasons match the model's actual weights," I want a normative `audit.ecoa.adverse_action.*` schema covering at minimum `reasons[]` (the listed reasons), `feature_attributions` (model-side weights or SHAP-style attributions, when available), and `model_explanation_method` (the institution's named method for deriving listed reasons from model output). Without it, the chain proves the bank said reason X; it does not bind reason X to what the model weighted.

**Redaction discipline is not in the spec body.** The spec body returns zero hits for `redact`, `tokeniz`, `de-identif`. customer-dispute-procedures.md §326-340 names a "captured JSON" form and an "Article 15 disclosure" form, and §10.11 uses `output_hash` to bind translated text "without binding the PII." That's a hash-anchor pattern, but it's deployed only on the translation entry. For the underlying model-call entry, the spec's posture is that the captured JSON is the content-bearing form (§5.2) — which means full PII is in the chain unless the institution has its own redaction layer. §4.4 says "OTLP-collector transformation pass-through (normative)" forbids collectors from mutating chain pipeline events; the implication is that redaction must happen pre-MAC at the SDK or post-MAC on a sidecar copy. Neither posture is normated. For a CFPB examiner, the auditable question is "what did you redact, where, and how do I verify the redaction did not conceal a decision driver" — and the spec leaves that institution-side. A normative `audit.redaction.*` schema (what was redacted, the redaction policy version, the field-level disposition) bound under the per-event MAC would close it.

**Consumer-side IKM access is named but the operational shape is fragile.** customer-dispute-procedures.md §"IKM access for customer-side verification" lists two acceptable shapes: protective-order disclosure and HSM-mediated verification. The doc says the institution "MUST name which shape it operates and the threshold at which it shifts." Good. But the spec body §7 step 7 fail-mode for an unknown `key_version` is `unknown key_version: no IKM for (tenant=T, key_version=V) at seq N` — and there is no normative path for the consumer's expert to surface that failure to the institution mid-verification short of escalating the dispute. Witness mode (§7) skips the IKM-bound steps and produces `Status: PASS-STRUCTURALLY, key-bound verification skipped`, which is real integrity property but not a full per-event MAC verification. A consumer's lawyer reading "PASS-STRUCTURALLY" should not have to litigate to find out whether the per-event MAC also verifies. The spec should normate a Bureau-facing or court-facing escalation path beyond the institution's CC8.1 self-naming.

### Nits (cosmetic / wording / cross-reference)

- **§10.11 attribute table** lists `audit.ecoa.translation.delivery_timestamp` as RECOMMENDED. For a CFPB examiner running the 30-day window check that the same doc cites at customer-dispute-procedures.md §109, this should be REQUIRED on any translation entry where `delivery_method` is also recorded. The two together are the within-window evidence; making one REQUIRED and the other RECOMMENDED leaves the window-check optional in practice.
- **customer-dispute-procedures.md §259** says "CFPB examiners use the same verifier the FFIEC examiners use; the artifact is identical. The handbook-mapping doc could add an explicit CFPB section in v1.1." Since v1.0a is the locked form and the close-out directive collapsed v1.1 candidates into v1.0a companion docs, this sentence is now stale — either the CFPB section is in `regulator-pack/` already (I did not locate it) or this sentence should drop the v1.1 hedge.
- **§13 stakeholder navigation** has an entry for "FFIEC consumer-protection / CFPB examiner" that points only to customer-dispute-procedures.md. A CFPB examiner reading the spec for the first time also needs §10.11 and §5.2 in that nav bullet — the integrity-of-evidence and translation-discipline anchors I will cite in any examination report.

## Strengths

The §5.2 best-evidence posture is the strongest piece of writing I have read on this question in any logging substrate. Naming the captured JSON as content-bearing and the canonical bytes as integrity-bearing — both originals under FRE 1001(d) — gives the Bureau exactly what it needs for a CID response: produce both, name which form answers which question, and let the cryptography carry the integrity claim while the JSON carries the human-readable narrative.

The §10.11 chained translation step is novel in industry practice and closes a real customer-side repudiation gap. The four-piece evidence package customer-dispute-procedures.md describes (chain integration, parent linkage, documented schema, MRM-review link) is a defensible operational shape.

The witness-mode verifier (§7) and the explicit "the institution does not gate access to the verifier binary or the §7 procedure" commitment in the EU-extensions section are exactly the asymmetric-evidence-balancing posture the Bureau looks for. A consumer-side expert can verify integrity without IKM access. That's not common.

The selective-production-and-sampling.md doc with its 18 USC §2703(d) partial-disclosure mode is an under-advertised capability — the same primitive serves a CID with the right cover letter.

## Questions to the spec authors

1. **(§10.11, missing FCRA parallel)** Is there a planned `audit.fcra.*` event family — analogous to §10.11's `audit.ecoa.translation.*` schema — that captures the §611 reinvestigation timing anchors (dispute received, additional info received, furnisher notified, reinvestigation completed, consumer notified)? If not, what's the institution's anchor today for proving the 30/45-day clock was met?

2. **(customer-dispute-procedures.md §"CFPB-specific procedures", §415)** The customer-correlation index is the load-bearing retrieval substrate for any CID, but I cannot find normative spec text on its integrity. Are CUEC entries themselves chain entries? If not, what stops a bank from regenerating the index at production time to omit consumers it would rather not surface?

3. **(§10.11 vs the underlying adverse-action entry)** The translation step has a normative eight-attribute schema; the underlying `audit.ecoa.adverse_action.*` payload that the translation binds to does not. Is a normative reasons schema (with feature attributions or model-explanation method) on the v1.0a or v1.0b roadmap, or is that intentionally institution-defined?

4. **(§5.2, §4.4 collector pass-through)** Where does redaction live in the conformant pipeline? §4.4 forbids collector mutation of chain attributes; §5.2 names the captured JSON as the content-bearing form. If a bank operates a redaction layer, is it pre-MAC at the SDK (in which case the chain captures redacted content and the consumer cannot reproduce the decision from the chain alone) or post-MAC on a sidecar (in which case the chain captures unredacted PII)? A normative posture statement and an `audit.redaction.*` schema would let the Bureau audit redaction without inferring it from architecture.

5. **(§7 witness mode, customer-dispute-procedures.md §"IKM access")** A consumer's expert running witness mode and reporting `PASS-STRUCTURALLY, key-bound verification skipped` does not know whether the per-event MAC would also verify. What's the spec's posture for surfacing that ambiguity to a non-technical consumer or a court? Specifically, would the working group support a normative "Bureau-mediated verification" shape where the Bureau holds the IKM under a standing protective order and runs the full §7 verification on the consumer's behalf?

6. **(§10.11 delivery_timestamp)** Why is `audit.ecoa.translation.delivery_timestamp` RECOMMENDED rather than REQUIRED, given that customer-dispute-procedures.md §109 cites it as the within-window anchor?
