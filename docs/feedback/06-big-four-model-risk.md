# 06 — Big Four Model Risk Management Senior Director (round 9)

> **Role.** SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12 model-risk lens applied to AI-agent decision capture. First-look review of the v1.0-rework corpus.

## Persona — Dr. Pradeep Iyer

**Background.** Senior Director in a Big Four firm's Model Risk Management practice. Eighteen years on SR 11-7 model-validation engagements at large US bank holding companies — credit, market, operational, AML, and (more recently) AI/ML. I author the firm's internal-position paper on AI-agent model risk and I sit on the working group that the firm uses to align its AI-MRM methodology with the Treasury AI RMF (Feb 2026), the OCC's emerging supervisory posture, and the EU AI Act Article 12 record-keeping obligation.

**Reading angle.** Model-risk substance, not cryptographic novelty. Three concurrent tests on every artifact:

1. Does the captured evidence support the SR 11-7 lifecycle — development testing, ongoing monitoring, effective challenge, and model retirement — for AI agents whose "model" is a probabilistic LLM call inside a longer agentic flow?
2. Does the evidence reconstruct the *decision*, not just the *event* — i.e. given a contested outcome two years later, can a validator reproduce the decision path well enough to evaluate it under SR 11-7's effective-challenge standard?
3. Do the materials given to the MRM committee accurately describe what the chain proves and what it does not, so the committee's oversight is calibrated to the actual control?

I am explicitly reading the corpus as a senior director who would have to defend the AI-MRM program in front of an OCC examiner, and who would have to sign the firm's annual position on whether the chain is a load-bearing input to the institution's AI model-risk framework.

### Questions

**Q1. Is the `gen_ai_parameters` attribute (model temperature, top_p, seed, etc.) sufficient for SR 11-7 reproducibility — i.e. can a validator pull a chain entry two years later and re-run the agent's LLM call closely enough to evaluate the decision under effective challenge?**

**Status:** Partial

Spec §4.4 introduces `ffiec.chain.gen_ai_parameters` as an optional attribute, defined as "JCS-canonical JSON of model sampling parameters (temperature, top_p, seed, etc.) for SR 11-7 reproducibility." That sentence is the right intent, and the JCS canonicalization choice means the parameter blob lands inside the MAC input — it is integrity-bearing, not advisory. Two implementations producing different parameter encodings cannot agree on the `payload_hash`. That is the property a validator needs.

Where my pencil hovers is on what `gen_ai_parameters` is required to *contain* for SR 11-7 reproducibility to hold. The spec says "temperature, top_p, seed, etc." and stops. SR 11-7 §V (model implementation) and §VI (ongoing monitoring) both depend on the validator being able to re-run the model in question. For a deterministic GLM that means the coefficients and the input vector. For an LLM call inside an agent flow, the reproducibility surface is materially larger:

- **Decoding parameters.** temperature, top_p, top_k, repetition_penalty, presence_penalty, frequency_penalty, max_tokens, stop sequences. Some of these the spec names; several it does not.
- **Sampling determinism inputs.** seed (named), but also the sampler implementation identifier — a vendor's "temperature 0 + seed 42" is not portable across vendors and is not even guaranteed reproducible across model-version updates within one vendor. The chain needs to capture the vendor and model version against which the seed was applied.
- **Model identifier.** The OTel GenAI semconv (`gen_ai.request.model`, `gen_ai.response.model`) covers this and the spec inherits it via the OTel envelope (§5 names `gen_ai.*` as covered by the MAC). I would expect a normative statement that the response.model is required for SR 11-7 reproducibility, not optional, because vendors silently re-route calls between model versions during outages and the validator needs to know which version actually answered.
- **System prompt and tool catalog hash.** Two LLM calls with identical user prompts, identical decoding parameters, and identical seeds will produce different outputs if the system prompt or the available tool catalog drifts. SR 11-7 reproducibility for an agent requires the validator to know the system-prompt content and the tool catalog at decision time. The chain would either capture them in `audit.*` (and let the MAC bind them) or capture content hashes with the underlying content stored in the institution's prompt-version registry.
- **Retrieval context.** For RAG-flavoured agent calls, the retrieved documents are part of the model input. Their identity and content are part of the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface. The chain has no field for this.
- **Tool-call results that fed the LLM.** In a multi-step agent, the LLM call under review may have been conditioned on outputs from earlier tool calls in the same run. The chain captures those as separate events (good — `seq` ordering preserves the sequence), but the validator needs the `gen_ai_parameters` block to declare which earlier `seq` values' outputs were inputs to this LLM call. Otherwise the validator reconstructs the temporal order but not the data-dependency graph.

The first three items are tractable extensions to `gen_ai_parameters` or to companion attributes; I would expect a v1.1 spec patch to expand the field's documented contents and to make `gen_ai.request.model` plus `gen_ai.response.model` required (not optional) for any chain entry whose `audit.*` payload represents a model call. The fourth and fifth items (system prompt, retrieval context) would likely live under `audit.*` per institution convention rather than under `ffiec.chain.*`, and the spec should state where the institution is expected to put them.

The sixth item (intra-run data-dependency) is the one that reaches deepest into SR 11-7 reproducibility — see Q2.

The MRM-committee brief (`docs/MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF.md`) frames this honestly in §"How this fits SR 11-7": *"Validators can pull a specific decision from the chain, including the prompt, the model response, and the parameters (when captured), and reproduce or evaluate it."* The parenthetical "when captured" is doing real work. A MRM committee reading the brief should understand that the chain captures *integrity over what was logged*, and the institution's AI-MRM program is responsible for ensuring that what was logged is sufficient for SR 11-7 reproducibility. That layering is correct, but the brief could be more explicit that the institution's AI-model inventory and the chain's `audit.*` schema are the load-bearing decisions for SR 11-7 reproducibility, not the chain construction itself.

So: the cryptographic substrate is sound, the field name is the right name, and the field is integrity-bound. The spec underspecifies what the field must contain for SR 11-7 to be satisfied, and the MRM committee brief understates how much of the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface lives in the institution's adjacent decisions rather than in the chain.

**Q2. Does the `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` / `dag_parents` linkage support post-hoc reconstruction of multi-step agent decisions to the standard a validator needs under SR 11-7 effective challenge?**

**Status:** Partial

Spec §4.4 introduces three optional attributes — `parent_run_id`, `parent_seq`, `dag_parents` — and `docs/design/02-chain-construction.md` §8.1 names three patterns: per-process runs with parent linkage (preferred), shared `run_id` with explicit re-key handoff, and DAG-shaped flows with multiple parents. The mutual-exclusion rule (parent linkage XOR DAG parents) is correct and the design doc commits a DAG test vector to the conformance corpus.

For an MRM validator reconstructing a multi-step decision, the linkage gives the necessary primitive: starting from any decision event, the validator can walk backward through parent edges to reach all contributing chain entries across processes. That is the structural property SR 11-7 effective challenge requires.

Three concerns sit between the primitive and the property:

1. **Linkage integrity.** `parent_run_id`, `parent_seq`, and `dag_parents` are listed as `optional` attributes in §4.4 and are explicitly enumerated in the §5 canonical-form exclusion rule as fields the MAC does NOT cover. Reading §5 again to be sure: *"The canonical bytes input to the MAC MUST EXCLUDE every chain-stamp field — that is, every attribute listed in §4.4 under the `ffiec.chain.*` namespace (specifically `prev_hash`, `payload_hash`, `key_version`, `key_fingerprint`, `format_version`, `mac_computed_at_utc`, `kms_handle_uri`, `algorithm`, plus the `seq` and the linkage fields `parent_run_id`, `parent_seq`, `dag_parents`)."* That excludes the linkage fields from the MAC input. I understand the rationale — the chain-stamp fields are the verifier's structural inputs and the MAC must not self-reference — but the linkage fields are different in kind. They are not structural inputs to the per-event MAC (the MAC only cares about prev_hash within one run); they are *substantive evidence about the agent's decision graph*. Excluding them from the MAC means an attacker who can write to the ledger after capture but before seal can rewrite the parent linkage without breaking the per-event MAC.

   The Merkle seal still detects the tampering (the seal commits to all `payload_hash` values for the day, and changing the linkage attribute would change the OTel attribute set and therefore the canonical bytes — wait, no: the linkage fields are excluded from the canonical bytes per the rule above, so changing them does *not* change the `payload_hash`, so the daily Merkle root does *not* commit to the linkage). That is the gap. The chain integrity property covers `payload_hash` continuity within a run; it does *not* cover the parent-edge graph across runs. A validator who walks the parent edges two years later is walking attacker-influenceable metadata if a server-side compromise occurred between capture and seal.

   The fix is small: add the linkage fields to the MAC input. The verification cost is identical (the verifier already computes the MAC) and the property gain — that the parent edges are integrity-bound under the same per-event MAC — is exactly what an MRM validator needs. If there is a reason the linkage fields belong outside the MAC input I have not reasoned through, the spec should say what it is, because the default reading is that this is an oversight.

2. **Cross-process atomicity.** In the shared-`run_id` handoff pattern (§8.1.2), both processes share `run_id` and the receiving process re-keys under its own `session_key_id` (referenced by the design doc as `session_key_id`, though the v1.0-rework vocabulary uses `key_version` — the design doc uses an older term that should be reconciled in a docs pass). The handoff event "carries the previous process's last `payload_hash`." That is the structural link, but the spec does not normatively define the handoff event's *schema* — what fields it carries beyond the structural prev_hash, how the verifier knows it is a handoff event vs a regular event, what the failure mode is when the handoff event is missing or malformed. For SR 11-7 reproducibility this matters because the handoff is the moment the model-risk validator is most exposed: if Process A produced 17 events and Process B produced 23 events under the same run_id, and the handoff event between them is corrupted, the validator's reconstruction of the decision is ambiguous in a way the chain construction does not surface.

3. **DAG topology validation.** Spec §4.4 defines `dag_parents` as a comma-separated string of `(run_id, seq)` pairs. That parses, but the verifier procedure in §7 does not include a step that validates DAG topology — i.e. that every `(run_id, seq)` named in a `dag_parents` attribute resolves to an existing chain entry in the same tenant's ledger. An MRM validator reconstructing a DAG-shaped agent flow would assume the verifier had checked this; the §7 procedure does not. The result is that a malformed `dag_parents` attribute (typo, missing parent, parent in another tenant) is silently accepted by the verifier and surfaces only when the MRM validator tries to walk the graph — at which point the validator is two years downstream of the capture and has no operational evidence to investigate. A new verifier step (call it §7 step 13: linkage resolution check) would close this; it would be optional under `--strict` and reportable as an anomaly under non-strict.

These are not blockers for a v1.0 reading. They are the items I would flag in the firm's AI-MRM position paper as "the chain handles in-run integrity correctly; the institution must operate compensating controls for cross-run linkage integrity until the spec normatively binds the linkage fields under the MAC." The compensating control is an institution-side ETL job that periodically verifies linkage resolvability and emits an operational event — straightforward to build, but the institution is doing work the spec could remove with a one-line change to the §5 exclusion rule.

The MRM-committee brief should acknowledge that multi-process and DAG-shaped agent flows are within scope and that the chain links them, but that the full integrity binding of the linkage graph is on the v1.1 roadmap. Right now the brief does not mention multi-process or DAG flows at all, which understates what the chain handles and overstates what the chain *integrity-binds*.

**Q3. Do the MRM-committee briefing materials (`docs/MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF.md`) accurately describe the new defensive primitives — per-tenant HKDF binding, `key_fingerprint` identity check, server-side Merkle+HSM rollup — at a level a chair without a cryptographic background can use to oversee the control?**

**Status:** Gap

The brief is well-written for what it covers, but the v1.0-rework's defensive primitives are not visible in it. The brief lists the four primitives in §"The four primitives (short version)":

1. HMAC chain at capture
2. Daily Merkle seal
3. HSM-rooted root signature
4. OpenTelemetry-native wire

That list is technically accurate but it predates the substantive rework that produced the v1.0-rework spec. Three rework items materially affect what the MRM committee should understand about the control:

1. **Per-tenant HKDF binding.** The construction now derives a session key per tenant by binding `tenant_id` into HKDF's `info` parameter (spec §4.1, inviolate property #1). Two tenants whose IKMs are accidentally swapped — operator typo, restored backup pointed at the wrong row, KMS misconfiguration — produce verifiably different session keys. For an MRM committee the relevant property is: cross-tenant chain-of-custody confusion is structurally prevented by the construction, not just operationally avoided. That is a meaningful change to what the committee should understand the chain protects against, and it is invisible in the brief's current text.

2. **`key_fingerprint` identity check.** Every chain entry now stamps a 16-byte `key_fingerprint = SHA-256(utf8(tenant_id) || ikm)[:16]` and the verifier asserts the looked-up IKM produces this fingerprint *before* computing any MAC (spec §7 step 8). For the MRM committee, the committee-relevant framing is: a botched key rotation that re-uses `key_version=1` for a different IKM is detected at lookup time with a precise message (`key_fingerprint mismatch at seq N`), not buried in a MAC-mismatch storm that looks like a generic chain failure. That changes the operational-evidence story the committee oversees — the chain produces actionable failures rather than ambiguous ones — and it changes what the committee asks the chain-ops team to demonstrate (per §"What the MRM Committee should ask" item 4: "How are master-key rotation and key-version transitions managed?"). The reconciliation procedure in spec §10.1 and audit procedure P-6 (`docs/audit-procedures.md`) are the operational evidence trail. None of this is in the brief.

3. **Server-side Merkle+HSM rollup placement.** Spec §4.2 and §4.3 explicitly state where each primitive lives: the Merkle seal lives at the ledger server, on the bank's perimeter; the HSM root signature lives in the HSM. The brief's primitive list describes what the primitives do but not where they live. The "where they live" question is what an MRM committee chair needs to ask of the institution's vendor and BYOC arrangements — the primitives are load-bearing only if their custody placement is correct, and a vendor-hosted topology where the HSM lives in the vendor's perimeter is materially different from a BYOC topology where the HSM lives in the bank's perimeter. The brief doesn't surface this.

The brief also doesn't surface three other rework items the MRM committee should understand:

- **MAC-IS-payload_hash.** Spec §4.1 inviolate property #6 makes "the MAC is the chain entry, persisted byte-for-byte" normative. The brief's primitive #1 says "Each event is cryptographically tied to the event before it"; the v1.0-rework binding is stronger — the bytes that carry the integrity property are the bytes the verifier compares. That is the difference between "the chain has a hash" and "the chain has a verifiable signature on every entry." For a committee chair, the headline is that the chain produces evidence the chain itself cannot discard.

- **Compile-time exclusion of the software-key adapter.** Spec §10.7 forbids a misconfigured production deployment from bringing the software-key adapter online. For the MRM committee, the relevant framing is that "dev-mode key material accidentally in production" is structurally prevented at build time, not just operationally caught after the fact. The committee chair would otherwise reasonably ask "what stops a misconfigured build from running with a development key?" and the answer should be in the brief.

- **Constant-time comparison.** Spec §10.8 binds the verifier to constant-time comparison primitives. For a committee chair this is a one-line check item ("the verifier uses constant-time comparison; no side-channel exposure during verification"); it is not in the brief.

The brief's framing in §"What this changes for MRM" is good and I would not change the structure. The fix is to extend §"The four primitives (short version)" with a paragraph or two on the v1.0-rework defensive primitives, in committee-readable language. A draft I would propose for a round-10 edit:

> The v1.0-rework specification adds three defensive primitives the committee should understand. First, the chain binds the tenant identifier into the cryptographic key-derivation step, so two tenants' chain entries are not interchangeable even if an operator accidentally points one tenant at another's key material. Second, every chain entry carries a 16-byte tenant-key fingerprint that the verifier checks before any cryptographic comparison; a botched key rotation produces a precise, named failure rather than an ambiguous one. Third, the daily seal is computed by the ledger server (on the bank's perimeter or its vendor's per-tenant-segregated equivalent) and signed by a hardware key the server itself cannot extract; the seal exists to detect tampering by the server and is therefore unforgeable by the server.

That paragraph plus a note in §"What the MRM Committee should ask" item 4 ("the chain-ops team should be able to walk the committee through the key-fingerprint reconciliation report from `docs/audit-procedures.md` P-6, including any unmatched fingerprints during the period") would close the gap.

**Q4. Do the customer-dispute procedures (`docs/customer-dispute-procedures.md`) use the chain evidence appropriately given the rework — i.e. does the procedure actually use the new defensive primitives where they are load-bearing, or does it treat the chain as a generic logging system?**

**Status:** Partial

The procedures doc is well-organised and the customer-side communication template is appropriately calibrated. The chain is used appropriately for the two headline customer-protection scenarios: the bank can produce an integrity-bearing record for a customer dispute (Option A, B, C in §"How do I know the bank didn't tamper with this record?"), and the verifier path supports independent verification by the customer's expert (§"Customer-side verification path"). Both are correctly framed.

Two procedural concerns:

1. **Adverse-action notices (ECOA) — `gen_ai_parameters` and reproducibility.** The procedures doc's §"Adverse-action notices (ECOA)" describes the chain providing the AI's response which the bank's compliance team translates into ECOA-compliant adverse-action language. That is correct as far as it goes. The model-risk concern is that the customer's advocate (or the CFPB on the customer's behalf) is increasingly likely to ask not just "what did the AI say" but "would the AI have said the same thing on a different day" — which is a reproducibility question, not a logging-integrity question. With `gen_ai_parameters` integrity-bound under the chain, the bank has the substrate to answer that question, but the procedures doc doesn't say so. A paragraph noting that the chain captures the model's decoding parameters (where the institution has configured `gen_ai_parameters` capture) and supports the institution's ability to re-run the decision under the same parameters would close this for the customer-dispute audience.

   The adjacent concern: when the bank's compliance team translates the AI's response into ECOA language, that translation is itself a model-risk-relevant decision. The procedures doc says "the translation is itself logged (the institution's compliance procedure documents the translation)." For SR 11-7 purposes, the translation should be a chain entry too, with the AI's original response as the parent. The procedures doc doesn't say whether the institution is expected to chain the translation step or to log it outside the chain. That choice has model-risk implications — a translation logged outside the chain is repudiable in the same way a vendor's logs are repudiable, which is exactly the gap the chain exists to close. The procedures doc should surface the choice and the MRM committee should opine on it.

2. **Customer-side verification — step 8 fingerprint check.** §"Customer-side verification path" describes the customer's expert running the verifier against a bank-produced ledger snapshot using the bank's public key. Correct. The verifier under v1.0-rework executes the §7 ordered procedure, including step 8 (key_fingerprint check). For the procedure to work, the customer's expert needs the IKM (or the equivalent verifier-side key material). The procedures doc doesn't address what the customer's expert receives that lets the verifier execute step 8. Two acceptable shapes exist: the bank discloses the IKM under appropriate protective order (which `docs/legal-disclosure.md` §"Court-ordered master-key disclosure" addresses for the master-key case but not specifically for customer dispute), or the bank provides an HSM-mediated verification path where the verifier dispatches HMAC operations through the HSM API without the IKM ever being disclosed. The procedures doc should describe which shape the institution operates and the MRM committee should understand the choice.

   Without that, a customer's expert running the verifier would hit step 7 (`unknown key_version: no IKM for (tenant=T, key_version=V)`) and the verification would report `structurally consistent, key-bound verification skipped` per spec §7 fail-closed semantics. That outcome is technically a successful structural verification, but it does not give the customer's expert what they came for — and the procedures doc, by not addressing the IKM-disclosure path for customer disputes, leaves the institution's customer-service team in the position of explaining the gap mid-dispute.

The chain construction is sound, the integrity property is real, and the procedures doc gets the major shape right. The two items above are documentation gaps that would surface during the first contested dispute and that an MRM committee should want closed before the dispute happens, not after.

**Q5. Does the AI-policy-alignment doc (`docs/regulator-pack/ai-policy-alignment.md`) reflect current and emerging policy — Treasury AI RMF, EU AI Act, DORA — at the level a senior MRM director would defend in front of an OCC examiner asking about cross-jurisdictional alignment?**

**Status:** Partial

The doc covers a wide policy surface and the alignment framings are mostly correct. Three concerns:

1. **Treasury AI RMF (Feb 2026) — undersurfaced.** The doc has a one-paragraph §"U.S. Treasury AI Risk Management Framework (Feb 2026)" which says only that "Logging integrity is a specific expectation; the chain satisfies it." That is true and the spec §11 references the Treasury framework as an informative reference. For an MRM director defending the alignment in front of an OCC examiner, the Treasury framework's specific expectations around AI model risk — model inventory, model lifecycle controls, third-party model governance, ongoing monitoring — are exactly the SR 11-7 lifecycle I am responsible for, and the chain composes with those expectations in ways the doc does not articulate. A subsection enumerating the Treasury RMF expectations and where the chain composes (model inventory: chain captures every decision, supporting model-inventory completeness checks; model lifecycle: chain output supports validation, ongoing monitoring, and retirement evidence; third-party governance: chain output is independent of vendor cooperation) would land this for the MRM audience.

2. **EU AI Act Article 12 — correct but understates Article 14 and Article 26.** The doc's table identifies Article 12 (record-keeping) as the headline, which is right. The table's row on Article 14 (human oversight) says "Chain captures AI decisions for human review." That is true but understates the Act's specific expectation: Article 14 requires that the human oversight be effective, which means the human reviewer needs sufficient information to second-guess the AI. The chain's ability to capture model parameters, prompt, and response (via `gen_ai_parameters` and `audit.*`) is the substrate that makes Article 14 oversight effective; the doc could surface that.

   Article 26 (obligations of deployers) is missing from the table entirely. For a financial-services deployer of a high-risk AI system, Article 26 imposes specific obligations including monitoring, logging, and informing affected persons. The chain composes with all three, and the doc should say so.

3. **DORA — Article 17 timing reference is correct but the chain's role in DORA's Article 6 (ICT risk-management framework) and Article 8 (identification) is not surfaced.** The doc's three-bullet DORA section names Articles 5, 17, and 28. Article 6 (ICT risk-management framework) and Article 8 (identification of ICT-supported business functions) are where the chain's broader composition with the institution's ICT risk program lives. For an MRM director coordinating with the institution's ICT risk function, the chain is one component of the broader framework, not a standalone control; the doc should make that composition explicit.

Three items the doc handles well: the EU AI Act / EBA / DORA / NIS2 stack is correctly framed as composing rather than competing with the FFIEC framework, the cross-jurisdictional consideration paragraph is the right framing for institutions operating multi-jurisdictionally, and the policy-stability paragraph at the end correctly identifies that the chain's primitive set is durable across policy evolution.

One item the doc does not address that I would expect: **OCC's emerging supervisory posture on AI**. The OCC has been signalling — through speeches, supervisory letters to specific institutions, and joint statements with the Fed and FDIC — increasing focus on AI model-risk governance. The doc references SR 11-7 and OCC Bulletin 2011-12 (the foundational MRM guidance) and the Treasury AI RMF, but does not address the bridge between them — i.e. how the OCC is operationalising AI model-risk supervision under SR 11-7 / 2011-12 in advance of formal new guidance. A paragraph noting that the chain's evidence is increasingly pertinent to OCC AI examinations under the existing MRM framework (no new rule required) would help the MRM director audience defend the chain's adoption decision in OCC interactions.

The doc is closer to complete than not, and the gaps above are extensions rather than corrections. None of them affect the chain's actual technical alignment with the named frameworks; they affect how the MRM director can use the doc in front of a regulator.

## Cross-cutting observations

Three observations cut across the questions and matter for the MRM committee chair audience the brief targets:

**The chain is a logging-integrity control, not a model-validation control — and the corpus is consistent on this.** The MRM-committee brief, the management summary, the audit-committee summary, and the AI-policy-alignment doc all hold this line: the chain produces integrity-bearing evidence; SR 11-7 effective challenge operates on that evidence. That layering is correct and the corpus does not overclaim. My Partial / Gap ratings are about *how* the chain composes with SR 11-7, not about whether it composes.

**The rework's defensive primitives are more substantive than the committee-facing materials currently surface.** The per-tenant HKDF binding, the per-entry key fingerprint, the constant-time comparison discipline, the compile-time exclusion of the dev adapter, and the MAC-IS-payload_hash binding are all v1.0-rework strengths the MRM committee should understand. The audit-procedures doc and the spec articulate them well; the committee-brief documents do not. That is a documentation refresh, not a corpus rework.

**The model-risk reach of the chain depends on what `audit.*` carries, and the spec is intentionally silent on `audit.*` schema.** Spec §5 names `audit.*` as part of the canonical bytes the MAC covers — the chain integrity-binds whatever the institution puts there. For SR 11-7 reproducibility, what the institution puts there is the load-bearing decision. The MRM committee's oversight of the AI-MRM program should include a periodic review of the `audit.*` schema against the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface (system prompt content or hash, retrieval context, tool catalog, intra-run data dependencies). The chain construction does not constrain this and should not; the MRM committee's oversight cadence should pick it up. A sentence in the MRM-committee brief noting that the institution's `audit.*` schema is a committee-relevant decision would close this.
