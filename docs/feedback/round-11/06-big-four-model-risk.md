### Questions

**Q1. Is the `gen_ai_parameters` expanded content guidance in spec §4.4 (with the integrity binding via §5 and the OTel `gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model` framing) sufficient for SR 11-7 reproducibility — i.e., can a validator pull a chain entry two years downstream and re-run the agent's LLM call closely enough to evaluate it under effective challenge?**

**Status:** Answered

Spec §4.4 defines `ffiec.chain.gen_ai_parameters` as JCS-canonical JSON of model sampling and reproducibility parameters and then enumerates RECOMMENDED contents that, taken as a set, cover the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface for an LLM call inside an agent flow. The enumeration covers six categories:

- decoding parameters (temperature, top_p, top_k, seed, max_tokens, stop_sequences, presence_penalty, frequency_penalty, repetition_penalty);
- sampler implementation identifier (vendor + library version);
- model identifier and version, request-side AND response-side, with the explicit operational note that vendors silently re-route between model versions during outages — the OTel `gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model` semconv pair is named, and the closing sentence makes them SHOULD-be-present for chain entries representing a model call;
- system-prompt content or content-hash with reference to a prompt-version registry;
- retrieval context (RAG document IDs and content-hashes);
- intra-run data dependencies expressed as `(run_id, seq)` of earlier chain entries whose outputs fed this LLM call.

Each item is named at the right normative force ("RECOMMENDED contents for chain entries representing a model call" — strong enough to drive institutional schema decisions, deferential enough that the institution determines the schema). The closing sentence — *"For chain entries that represent a model call, `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` SHOULD be present (their integrity is bound through the OTel envelope per §5)"* — closes the model-routing concern that an OCC examiner asking "which model actually answered this call on the day in question" would otherwise leave open. Because §5 lists `gen_ai.*` and `tool.*` among the fields the MAC covers, every parameter the institution puts in `gen_ai_parameters` and every model identifier on the OTel envelope inherits the per-event MAC. That is the property a validator needs two years later: not just "the institution claims this was the model and these were the parameters," but "the institution committed at capture time to this being the model and these being the parameters, and any rewrite between capture and seal would have broken the MAC."

The MRM-committee brief picks this up correctly in its updated "What the MRM Committee should ask" item 3, with the parenthetical *"For SR 11-7 reproducibility, the chain integrity-binds whatever the institution puts in `audit.*` and `gen_ai_parameters`; the institution's `audit.*` schema is a committee-relevant decision and should be reviewed periodically against the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface."* That layering — the chain integrity-binds whatever is logged; the institution's schema decision is what is logged — is the load-bearing framing for the MRM committee. The brief gets it right and surfaces the institution-side decision as the committee's responsibility rather than absorbing it into the chain's claims.

The customer-dispute procedures translate the same substrate to the customer-protection audience with an institution-side response template that names model nondeterminism explicitly: *"Significant variation across re-runs (a phenomenon called model nondeterminism) is itself information our model-risk-management committee evaluates."* That naming matters. The MRM committee's reproducibility posture must acknowledge that re-runs may not produce byte-identical outputs even with the same inputs, and the chain's role is to bound the input-side reproducibility, not to manufacture output-side determinism the underlying model does not provide. The template gets this distinction right, which is what an MRM director needs the customer-dispute desk to reflect when an advocate presses on the difference between "what the AI said" and "what the AI would have said on a different day."

The audit-procedures doc closes the operability loop with P-25, addressed in Q5 below. The combination — spec §4.4 RECOMMENDED contents, §5 integrity binding, MRM-brief item 3 framing, customer-dispute reproducibility template, and audit P-25 testing — is what moves the chain from "logging-integrity control adjacent to SR 11-7" to "load-bearing input to SR 11-7 reproducibility." There is no remaining gap at this layer.

**Q2. Do the linkage-field changes (parent_run_id, parent_seq, dag_parents now INCLUDED in canonical bytes per spec §5; handoff event normative schema in design §8.1.2; verifier §4.3.1 step 13 DAG topology resolution) close the cross-process and DAG integrity gaps for SR 11-7 effective challenge of multi-step agent decisions?**

**Status:** Answered

Three coordinated changes that, taken together, close the cross-process integrity gap. I read each in turn.

**Spec §5 inclusion of linkage fields under the MAC.** The canonical-form section now lists *"The cross-run linkage fields: `parent_run_id`, `parent_seq`, `dag_parents`. These describe the event's relationship to other events in other runs and are substantive evidence about the agent's decision graph; they are not chain-stamp fields. Including them in the canonical bytes binds the cross-run linkage under the per-event MAC, so an attacker who can write to the ledger after capture but before seal cannot rewrite the parent linkage without breaking the MAC."* Design 02 §2.3 mirrors the rationale. This is the right change at the right place. Verification cost is unchanged — the verifier already computes the MAC; including three optional attributes in the canonical bytes does not add work — and the property gain is that the parent-edge graph is now integrity-bound under the same per-event MAC the rest of the substantive payload enjoys. A server-side compromise that rewrote linkage attributes between capture and seal would break the per-event MAC, which the verifier catches at §7 step 9. That is the property an SR 11-7 validator needs when reconstructing a multi-step decision two years downstream, and it is the property a CFPB enforcement attorney needs when arguing in front of a court that the chained translation of an adverse-action notice (Q4) was not invented after the original decision.

**Design 02 §8.1.2 normative handoff event schema.** The receiving process's first event in a shared-`run_id` handoff is now normatively defined with required fields `audit.handoff.from_run_id`, `audit.handoff.from_seq`, `audit.handoff.from_payload_hash`, `audit.handoff.handoff_at`, and an optional `audit.handoff.from_host`. The `audit.*` namespace placement means the fields land inside the canonical bytes per §5 and inherit the per-event MAC. The verifier behavior is normatively addressed: a first event without the `audit.handoff.*` fields is structurally indistinguishable from a regular event AS LONG AS its `prev_hash` matches the previous event's `payload_hash`, and a handoff event whose `audit.handoff.from_payload_hash` does not equal the entry's `prev_hash` is rejected at spec §7 step 6 (`chain link broken at seq N`). That correctly resolves the ambiguity case I would have flagged: the handoff is the moment a model-risk validator is most exposed (one process's commitments stop, another's commitments begin), and the spec now binds the handoff fields under the same integrity property as the rest of the chain, with the verifier's existing structural check catching mismatch.

**Verifier design §4.3.1 step 13 DAG topology resolution.** The verifier now adds an optional step 13 (anomaly under default; FAIL under `--strict`) that walks each event with `dag_parents`, parses the comma-separated `(run_id, seq)` pairs, and looks up each parent in the same tenant's ledger. A missing parent (typo in `dag_parents`, SDK persistence failure, network drop, parent in a different tenant) produces an anomaly under default mode and a FAIL with reason `dag_parents resolution failed` under `--strict`. The check is appropriately placed at the day level after step 10 because parent events may be in earlier days within the verification range. The PDF report's anomaly section in §5.1 lists `dag_parents resolution failures` as a reportable anomaly type, so the institution's anomaly-evaluation process picks it up through the existing template (`docs/anomaly-documentation-template.md`). The mutual exclusivity rule (single-parent linkage uses `parent_run_id`/`parent_seq`; DAG linkage uses `dag_parents`; the two are not combined on the same event, per spec §4.4 and design 02 §8.1.3) keeps the verifier's resolution logic unambiguous.

The combination — linkage fields under the MAC, normative handoff event schema, DAG topology resolution at step 13 — gives the SR 11-7 validator the structural primitive AND the integrity binding AND the verifier-side detection mechanism for the multi-process and DAG-shaped decision graphs that production agent platforms increasingly produce. There is no gap that requires institution-side compensating ETL or out-of-band reconciliation to close. For an MRM director defending the institution's reconstruction of a contested two-year-old multi-process decision, that completeness matters: the response is "we can walk you through the DAG from the chain alone," not "we can walk you through the per-process chain but the cross-process linkage is operationally reconstructed from a separate system whose integrity is not chain-bound."

This is the property that closes the "misconfigured environment flag in production" question that examiners have started asking after recent industry incidents where production systems ran with development credentials for extended periods.

- **Constant-time comparison discipline.** Short, accurate, and at the right level: *"Side-channel attacks against the verifier process are closed at the cryptographic-discipline layer; no exposure during verification."* The chair does not need to know what constant-time comparison is to understand that the residual risk is closed at the cryptographic layer rather than left as an institutional control.

The closing paragraph after the three properties names where each primitive lives (SDK on the application host for per-event integrity; ledger server with HSM signing in the bank's perimeter or per-tenant-segregated vendor equivalent for the daily integrity; HSM hardware key for the daily signature; regulator-held public key for the verifier's signature check) and characterises that placement as what makes the integrity claim load-bearing in vendor-hosted and BYOC topologies. Specifically: walk us through the most recent key-fingerprint reconciliation report (the `master.reconciliation_completed` operational event), including any fingerprint mismatches during the period and how they were resolved (audit-procedures P-6)."* Item 6 now adds *"For any `key_fingerprint mismatch` finding: was the IKM-roster row identified, the change-management approval recorded, and the verifier re-run against the corrected roster?"* — the four-piece evidence bar from the anomaly template, expressed as a question the chair can ask. Item 7 now points at the new IR playbook scenarios for `key_fingerprint mismatch` (Scenario 7), `unknown_key_version` (Scenario 8), and `audit_file.truncation_detected` (Scenario 9). The brief is now what an MRM committee chair needs to oversee the v1.0 control at the right level. The translation from a cryptographic property ("HKDF binds the tenant_id into the key derivation") to a chair-usable framing ("two tenants' chain entries are not interchangeable even if an operator accidentally points one tenant at another's key material") is what makes this section operationally useful. An MRM director can hand the brief to the chair without needing to brief over the cryptographic primitive set.

**Q4. Do the customer-dispute procedures (`docs/customer-dispute-procedures.md`) — specifically the ECOA chaining of the translation step and the IKM access shapes for customer-side verification — handle the chain evidence appropriately given v1.0's linkage fields under the MAC?**

**Status:** Answered

Two procedural items, both substantively closed.

**ECOA chaining of the translation step.** The procedures doc states *"The translation is itself a chain entry, with the AI's original response as the parent (per spec §4.4 `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` linkage). The MRM committee opines on whether the institution chains the translation step or logs it outside the chain; the recommended decision is to chain."* That is the right framing on three counts. First, it names the linkage primitive (`parent_run_id` / `parent_seq`) the institution uses to chain the translation, which makes the recommendation actionable rather than aspirational. Second, it cites the model-risk concern (a translation logged outside the chain is repudiable in the same way vendor logs are repudiable, which is exactly the gap the chain exists to close) in the language of the rest of the corpus. Third, it correctly identifies the decision as the MRM committee's, not the chain operations team's — the chain provides the substrate; the committee opines on the institution's posture.

The substantive integrity gain comes from the linkage-fields-under-the-MAC change in spec §5 (per Q2): the chained translation now inherits the per-event MAC integrity binding via `parent_run_id` and `parent_seq` in the canonical bytes. The translation step is no longer a hole in the institution's customer-dispute response. A CFPB enforcement attorney pursuing an ECOA matter who asks "show me that the adverse-action language the customer received was actually derived from the AI's response, not invented after the fact by the compliance team" can be walked through the linkage from the translation chain entry back to the AI-response chain entry, with both events under the per-event MAC, and the linkage itself under the MAC.

**IKM access for customer-side verification.** The new §"IKM access for customer-side verification" addresses the verifier-side failure mode that would otherwise surface in the first contested customer dispute. It names the two acceptable shapes — protective-order disclosure (litigation context) and HSM-mediated verification (operational context) — with the right thresholds for each:

> The institution's customer-dispute procedure MUST name which shape it operates and the threshold at which it shifts (typically: operational shape for CFPB inquiries and regulator complaints; protective-order shape only when the dispute escalates to litigation). Without naming the shape, the institution risks the customer's expert encountering `unknown_key_version` at step 7 mid-verification, which leaves the verification incomplete and the dispute unresolved.

That is precisely the failure mode an MRM director would have raised pre-emptively. Without IKM access, the verifier reports `structurally consistent, key-bound verification skipped` per spec §7 fail-closed semantics — technically a successful structural verification, but it does not give the customer's expert what they came for. The MUST-name-the-shape requirement, combined with the institutional-decision framing (the MRM committee opines on the chosen shape per its overall risk tolerance for IKM disclosure), correctly places the operational decision before the dispute starts rather than during it. The annual-review framing (the institution documents the decision in its control description and reviews it annually) keeps the decision alive in the institution's governance cycle.

The two shapes are well-chosen. The protective-order disclosure cross-references `docs/legal-disclosure.md` §"Court-ordered master-key disclosure," which is the conservative posture appropriate to litigation. The HSM-mediated verification shape (the customer's expert dispatches HMAC operations through the institution's HSM API for the affected runs without ever holding the IKM bytes; the expert's report attests to the verification using the institution's HSM as the cryptographic delegate) is the right operational shape for CFPB inquiries and regulator complaints because it preserves IKM custody while still producing a credible expert report. An MRM director can defend either shape in front of the institution's risk function; the spec correctly leaves the threshold to the institution's documented procedure rather than mandating one shape over the other.

The §"Reproducibility and the customer's 'would the AI have said the same thing on a different day' question" subsection (covered in Q1) closes the third procedural concern an MRM director would have raised — the customer's reproducibility question, distinct from the integrity question. The procedures doc is now what an MRM director would want their customer-dispute desk to operate from.
