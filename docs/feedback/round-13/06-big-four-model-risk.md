## Headline

This is a strong v1.0 bundle for what an SR 11-7 program needs from a logging-integrity control. The chain integrity-binds the cross-run linkage fields under the per-event MAC (spec §5 — `parent_run_id`, `parent_seq`, `dag_parents` are inside the canonical bytes), which means the agent's decision graph is itself substantive evidence the verifier rejects tampering on. The `gen_ai_parameters` row in §4.4 names the SR 11-7 reproducibility surface explicitly — decoding parameters, sampler implementation identifier, request-side AND response-side model identifiers (closing the silent-vendor-rerouting gap), system-prompt content or content-hash with prompt-version registry reference, retrieval context, intra-run data dependencies. The MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF question 3 composes the per-model decision-count distribution with the institution's MRM model-inventory completeness check, which is the right composition — a model producing decisions but absent from the inventory is a coverage gap; an inventory entry producing no chain-captured decisions is potentially decommissioned-but-still-listed. The customer-dispute procedures' variation-threshold guidance establishes the decision-equivalence threshold in advance with per-decision-class tiers (95% for high-stakes denial, 80% for routing) and a customer-impact-tier indicator on the `audit.*` payload, which is exactly what the MRM committee needs so the customer-dispute desk does not improvise during a dispute. The verifier-design step-13 DAG-topology resolution check turns a missed-parent-event from a downstream SR 11-7 reconstruction failure into an anomaly the auditor sees in the report.

I cannot pass the bundle at 0/0 this round. Three issues block. The first is a normative-vs-MRM-committee-brief drift: the MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF question 3 names per-model decision-count composition with the institution's model inventory but the audit-procedures menu carries no procedure for testing that composition during a SOC or internal-audit period — P-25 covers `gen_ai_parameters` schema completeness but not the inventory cross-check. The second is the variation-threshold guidance in `customer-dispute-procedures.md` does not name the procedure that produces the reproduction evidence — the institution must re-run the decision under the captured inputs to evaluate variation, but neither the customer-dispute doc nor the audit-procedures menu names which team operates the reproduction, what evidence the reproduction produces, nor how the reproduction evidence is itself captured (chain-or-not is the institution's choice, but the chain-or-not decision is silent in the variation-threshold section). The third is a normative-strength asymmetry between the chain's `parent_run_id`/`parent_seq` integrity binding and the `gen_ai_parameters` reproducibility surface — the cross-run linkage fields are MAC-bound (§5 paragraph "The cross-run linkage fields"), but `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` are SHOULD-be-present (§4.4 closing sentence) for chain entries representing a model call, which leaves an MRM-program gap when the institution's chain operates without the model identifiers and the validator cannot satisfy SR 11-7 reproducibility against a chain that does not name the model that produced the decision.

**Verdict.** 1 gap, 2 partials. Each is mechanical to close. The bundle is otherwise the strongest chain-of-custody artifact set I have read for SR 11-7 alignment, and the MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF question 3 + customer-dispute variation-threshold pair is genuine novel work — most banks I review do not have a pre-established variation threshold and the customer-dispute desk improvises. The bundle ships that posture and the institution can adopt it.

## Observations (not findings)

**O-1.** The MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF "How this fits SR 11-7" section names four chain compositions: reproducibility, ongoing monitoring, independence, documentation. The section closes with "The chain does NOT satisfy SR 11-7 by itself. SR 11-7 is a model-validation framework; the chain is a logging-integrity control. The two compose; neither replaces the other." This is the right framing but it stops short of naming the SR 11-7-defined model-validation activities the chain does NOT support. A committee chair reading the brief asks the natural follow-up: "what about back-testing, sensitivity analysis, benchmarking against challenger models, and stability testing — does the chain support those?" The honest answer is partial: back-testing runs on chain-bearing data with the same integrity confidence as ongoing monitoring, sensitivity analysis runs against the captured `gen_ai_parameters` (the chain integrity-binds the inputs but does not run the sensitivity analysis), benchmarking runs against the chain's per-model decision distribution (composed against the model inventory, per the F-1 procedure), and stability testing runs on chain-bearing time-series. Naming these three additional compositions in the brief makes the SR 11-7 fit narrative complete. This is not a finding because the existing four compositions are the load-bearing ones for the committee's oversight role; the additional three are validator-team compositions the validator team operates without needing the committee's intervention.

**O-2.** The customer-dispute procedures' adverse-action notice section (lines 36–45) recommends chaining the translation step from AI response to ECOA-compliant adverse-action language, with the AI's original response as the parent. The same recommendation pattern would apply to two other compliance-translation steps the institution operates: (a) the UDAAP analysis translation (an AI flag → UDAAP-compliant disclosure), and (b) the disparate-impact-testing translation (an AI decision → fair-lending disclosure under ECOA Reg B). Both are CFPB-relevant translations the institution might log outside the chain (improving repudiation risk) or chain (closing repudiation per the same logic the adverse-action section applies). The customer-dispute doc references CFPB-specific procedures at lines 87–96 but does not name these two additional translations as candidates for the same chain-or-not decision. Adding them as a one-paragraph extension under the adverse-action section would make the chain's compliance-translation posture comprehensive across the consumer-finance regulatory surface. This is not a finding because the adverse-action case is the highest-frequency translation and the doc's pattern generalizes mechanically.

**O-3.** The MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF "Cost summary" section names $80k–$200k/year for a mid-size institution and points at `docs/cost-model.md`. From an MRM-program perspective, the cost-side question the committee should ask is not the all-in operating cost but the cost-of-NOT-operating-the-chain — that is, the institution's exposure when a customer dispute or a regulator inquiry produces a vendor-dependent narrative the institution cannot independently defend. The narrative form: "Without the chain, the institution's response to a CFPB inquiry about a specific AI-driven adverse-action notice depends on the vendor's continued cooperation; with the chain, the institution produces the integrity-bearing record directly and the response is one verifier-walk output away." This framing is implicit in the brief's "What this changes for MRM" table at lines 17–25 but not named in the cost section. A committee chair reading the cost section gets the operating cost; the cost-of-not-operating framing is what makes the operating cost defensible against a controller's challenge. Adding a sentence to the cost section ("The cost-of-not-operating framing is in `MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF.md` lines 17–25; the operating cost above is to be evaluated against that framing, not in isolation") would close the loop. This is not a finding because the framing is recoverable from the brief; surfacing it in the cost section would improve the brief's operability for a budget-constrained committee chair.

I then read `docs/audit-procedures.md` end to end. The "Procedures by control category" section at line 9 organises procedures into Authentication and access (P-1..P-3), Cryptography (P-4..P-6), Operations (P-7..P-10), Verifier and independent assessment (P-11..P-13), IR playbook (P-14..P-16), Vendor management (P-17..P-19), Anomaly evidence completeness (P-22..P-25), Configuration management (P-20..P-21). The procedure is mechanical to add. Modeled on P-25's shape:

```
**P-26. Model-inventory composition completeness.**

- Pull the chain's `gen_ai.response.model` distribution for the reporting period (count of chain entries per distinct response-model identifier)
- Pull the institution's MRM model inventory as of the period-end date (the institution's documented list of AI models in production with model-id, model-version, deployment-status, and inventory-entry-date)
- Join the two on (model-id, model-version) — note that the chain's `gen_ai.response.model` carries the response-side identifier (which captures vendor silent-rerouting), so the join must be on the response-side identifier
- For each chain-observed model NOT in the inventory: confirm the institution has a documented evaluation (typically: the model is a recent addition and the inventory has not yet caught up with provisioning, or the model is a vendor-routed alternate during a primary-model outage and the institution's vendor-routing policy covers the case)
- For each inventory model with zero chain-observed decisions: confirm the institution has a documented evaluation (typically: the model is decommissioned-but-still-listed pending the institution's quarterly inventory-cleanup cadence, or the model is in a non-production environment that does not chain)
- The MRM committee's documented evaluation of any unresolved cases is the institution's MRM-program evidence; gaps are MRM-program findings that the institution's model-inventory hygiene is weaker than the SR 11-7 framework requires
```

The procedure ships in audit-procedures.md as P-26 in the "Anomaly evidence completeness" section (renumbering the existing P-22..P-25 is not required because the section header already groups them; P-26 extends the group). The corresponding TSC mapping is CC8.1 (change management — the inventory is itself a change-managed artifact) plus CC7.5 (system change risk identification). No new CUEC is required because the existing CUEC-OPS-04 ("The institution operates a documented MRM model inventory at a documented cadence") covers the institution-side control; P-26 is the testing procedure for that CUEC.

The reproducibility section names the substrate: "The chain captures the model's decoding parameters (where the institution has configured `gen_ai_parameters` capture per spec §4.4) and supports the institution's ability to re-run the decision under the same parameters, the same model version (request-side AND response-side, captured per OTel `gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model`), the same system prompt content (captured in `audit.*` per institution convention), and the same retrieval context."

The variation-threshold section establishes the disposition: decision-equivalence threshold (same final decision), per-decision-class threshold tiers (95%+ for high-stakes; 80%+ for low-stakes), customer-impact tier sensitivity (chain-captured `audit.*` indicator).

The two sections compose a calibrated MRM control posture, but they leave three operability questions open.

**(a) Where does the reproduction run, and what does it produce as evidence?** The doc says "The institution's MRM team performs the reproduction; the chain integrity-binds the inputs that the reproduction needs." The reproduction itself — the sequence of re-run model calls — is silent on whether it captures into the chain. By analogy to the adverse-action notice translation at lines 36–45 (which the doc explicitly recommends chaining: "**The translation is itself a chain entry**, with the AI's original response as the parent (per spec §4.4 `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` linkage). This is the institution's choice but it is the recommended posture: a translation logged outside the chain is repudiable in the same way a vendor's logs are repudiable, which is exactly the gap the chain exists to close."), the reproduction should also be a chain entry with the original decision's `(run_id, seq)` as the parent. Then the reproduction is itself integrity-bound and a future challenge ("did the institution actually run the reproduction") is answered by the chain output.

**(b) What is the schema for the reproduction-result record?** The natural shape is:

```
audit.reproduction.original_run_id           — the disputed decision's run_id
audit.reproduction.original_seq              — the disputed decision's seq
audit.reproduction.rerun_count               — number of re-runs (e.g., 5 for high-stakes)
audit.reproduction.decision_equivalent_count — number of re-runs producing same final decision
audit.reproduction.threshold_applied         — "high-stakes:95%" or "low-stakes:80%" or institution-defined
audit.reproduction.threshold_disposition     — pass / fail-MRM-review / fail-customer-favorable-revisit
audit.reproduction.reviewer                  — MRM team member's institutional identifier
audit.reproduction.reviewed_at_utc           — RFC 3339 UTC timestamp of disposition
```

The schema is the institution's MRM-program design but the doc should name it as RECOMMENDED so an institution standing up the customer-dispute capability for the first time has a starting point. The same pattern applies that the MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF question 3 applies to `gen_ai_parameters`: the chain integrity-binds whatever the institution puts there, but the substrate-design discussion belongs in the customer-dispute doc.

**(c) How does a SOC engagement test that the institution actually operates the reproduction procedure when a dispute arrives?** No procedure consumes a reproduction-record set. A SOC engagement reviewing the institution's customer-dispute response capability has nothing to test against. Add a procedure (P-27 in the proposed P-26..P-27 extension) consuming the chain's `audit.reproduction.*` records for the period (one per dispute that triggered a reproduction), the corresponding customer-dispute case files, and the institution's MRM-team disposition. The four-piece evidence: (i) reproduction record exists for each dispute that the customer-dispute desk's case log records as triggering MRM review, (ii) the threshold applied matches the decision-class per the institution's pre-established tiers, (iii) the disposition matches the decision-equivalent-count vs the threshold, (iv) any customer-favorable-revisit disposition is followed by the institution's documented original-decision revisit (the disposition is not just recorded, it is acted on).

The fix is two paragraphs to `customer-dispute-procedures.md` (one for reproduction-chain posture as RECOMMENDED, one for reproduction-record schema) and a corresponding procedure in `audit-procedures.md` (P-27).

### F-3 evidence trail

I read `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §4.4 the `gen_ai_parameters` row's recommended-contents narrative and the §4.4 closing sentence.

The §4.4 row text reads (from the recommended-contents narrative) "model identifier and version (request-side AND response-side, per the OTel `gen_ai.request.model` / `gen_ai.response.model` semconv — vendors silently re-route between model versions during outages)". This is correct framing — vendor silent rerouting is a real-world failure mode (a request to a primary frontier-class model silently routed to a downscale alternate during a capacity event), and the response-side identifier is the integrity-bound evidence the validator uses to know which model actually served the decision.

The §4.4 closing sentence reads: "For chain entries that represent a model call, `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` SHOULD be present (their integrity is bound through the OTel envelope per §5)."

Two structural issues. **(i) SHOULD vs MUST.** A chain entry representing a model call without the model identifier is conformant under SHOULD. A validator pulling the chain entry to reproduce the decision under SR 11-7 has no model-identifier signal — the validator must guess from deployment topology, timestamp, and `audit.*` content. The §4.4 narrative names the silent-vendor-rerouting gap; SHOULD permits the gap to remain open. MUST closes it. **(ii) "Represents a model call" is institution-defined.** A chain entry's status as "represents a model call" is determined by the institution's `audit.*` schema convention or by the presence of OTel `gen_ai.*` attributes. Neither is a normative discriminator the spec defines. A validator reading the spec normatively does not know which chain entries should carry the identifiers, so cannot detect missing-identifier cases.

The fix is two-line. Replace the closing sentence with:

> For chain entries whose canonical bytes carry any OTel `gen_ai.*` attribute (per the OTel GenAI Semantic Conventions), `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` MUST be present. Their integrity is bound through the OTel envelope per §5; their absence on a chain entry carrying other `gen_ai.*` attributes is non-conformant.

Then add a §7 step between current step 12 (cadence/dev-mode) and the day-level summary:

> **Step 12a (model-identifier presence).** For each event in the day whose canonical bytes carry any OTel `gen_ai.*` attribute, assert that `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` are present. Mismatch → `gen_ai_model_identifier_missing at seq N`. The check is structural and does not require the IKM.

Add a corresponding row to design `02-chain-construction.md` §8.1 (verifier-side failures) and a corresponding row to design `07-verifier-design.md` per-event walk. Add the failure mode to `anomaly-documentation-template.md` line 28–39 enumeration with default severity Medium (not Critical: the chain entry's other content is integrity-protected; the missing identifier is a SR 11-7-program concern, not a chain-integrity concern). The MRM-COMMITTEE-BRIEF question 3 already covers the committee-side oversight of the schema; the verifier-side check ensures the schema-as-declared matches the chain-as-emitted.

The strength upgrade does not invalidate any existing chain entry whose canonical bytes carry no `gen_ai.*` attribute — the chain entry is conformant under the upgraded text exactly as it was under the original SHOULD. The upgrade only catches chain entries that carry `gen_ai.*` attributes WITHOUT the request/response model identifiers, which is the silent-vendor-rerouting evidence gap.
