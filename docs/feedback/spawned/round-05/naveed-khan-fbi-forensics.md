# Naveed Khan — FBI Cyber Division First-Look Review of FFIEC chain-of-custody v1.0a
**Date:** 2026-05-07
**Reviewer:** Naveed Khan, Senior Forensic Examiner, FBI CyD (NY Field Office)
**Posture:** First encounter with v1.0a; no prior iteration history. Reviewed from a federal criminal-investigation lens.

## What I look for in any chain-of-custody system

When I serve a Rule 41 warrant or a 2703(d) order on a financial institution, I bring back digital evidence. My job before it ever sees an AUSA's hand is to authenticate it under FRE 901(b)(9), get it self-authenticating under FRE 902(13)/(14) where I can, qualify it as a regularly conducted activity under FRE 803(6), and survive the best-evidence challenge under FRE 1001-1004 when the original — here, an LLM token stream — no longer exists. I also need to refute spoliation arguments and walk a grand jury through the evidence in plain English. The chain has to make all of those jobs easier, not harder.

The spec is the most rigorous private-sector chain-of-custody construction I've seen for AI evidence. It's testable, the math is FIPS-grounded, and it gives an expert witness a real Daubert posture. That's the headline. Most of my findings below are where the spec is one step short of producing artifacts I can put in front of a federal magistrate without follow-up work — not where the cryptography is weak.

## Findings

### Gaps (testimony or warrant scenarios the spec doesn't support)

**G-1. No FRE 902(13)/(14) certification template anywhere in the corpus.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §3.3, `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §10.13
**Investigation scenario:** I serve a Rule 41 warrant on a bank, get back a chain export, and want it to come into evidence at trial without a live custodian. FRE 902(13) (records generated by an electronic process) and 902(14) (data copied from an electronic device, by hash) eliminate live foundation testimony if the certification is correctly formed.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 902(13)/(14); the rule requires a sworn certification meeting 902(11) form. The litigation-support doc names 902(11) as the goal but supplies no template. Each institution ends up drafting its own and the AUSA learns at the suppression hearing whether it works.
**What I'd need to see:** A template certification under `docs/regulator-pack/` or `docs/legal-disclosure.md` that names: (a) the records by `(tenant_id, run_id, seq)` range, (b) the verifier binary's SHA-256 and the SLSA attestation, (c) the regular-course-of-business attestation per FRE 803(6), (d) the integrity hash of the ledger snapshot, (e) the public-key fingerprint, signed under penalty of perjury per 28 USC §1746. Should ship with the deployment package so AUSAs in MD/NY/IL/CA aren't each rolling their own.

**G-2. No procedure for forensic preservation by a federal investigator (vs. by the institution).**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §8.3, §10.4
**Investigation scenario:** I show up at the bank's data center with a search warrant. Section 10.4 covers the institution's voluntary handoff to "an examiner — law enforcement, defense counsel, neutral third party," but Rule 41 execution is not voluntary. I'm imaging storage, not asking for a digital handoff.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 901(b)(9) for the warrant return; the chain of custody for the seizure itself has to interlock with the chain of custody the spec creates. United States v. Ganias, 824 F.3d 199 (2d Cir. 2016) (en banc), constrains what I can keep and for how long; if I image more than the warrant authorizes, suppression is on the table.
**What I'd need to see:** A subsection — `docs/legal-disclosure.md` §"Search-warrant execution against the receiver" — that names: (a) what to image (ledger storage, seal records, IKM custody pointer, public-key registry entry, verifier binary) so the warrant-return is complete; (b) how to write-block during imaging without breaking append-only semantics that the spec's storage layer relies on; (c) the SHA-256 of the imaged volume captured at seizure under a witness; (d) what the institution's role is during execution (cooperative, observed, or compelled); (e) Ganias-compliant minimization for non-responsive tenant data when the ledger is multi-tenant. Right now an FBI agent improvises.

**G-3. The chain output is not by itself a 902(14) hash-authenticated copy.**
**Section/file:** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §6, §7
**Investigation scenario:** FRE 902(14) self-authenticates ESI when the data is "copied from an electronic device, storage medium, or file" and "authenticated by a process of digital identification." A defense lawyer challenges the bytes I produce as not being byte-identical to the bytes at the institution. The chain MAC proves the entry's content integrity — not that the file I'm holding is a faithful copy of the institution's storage.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 902(14). The spec gives me per-entry MAC, daily Merkle root, and HSM signature on the root. It does not give me a single integrity hash over the file-as-handed-over.
**What I'd need to see:** A specified "snapshot manifest" — a sidecar JSON or CMS envelope, signed by the institution's HSM, naming every file in the export bundle plus its SHA-256. The verifier already enforces `\n` termination per §4.1 and rejects truncation; the snapshot manifest closes the byte-level FRE 902(14) gap. Add to §10.13 evidentiary artifacts.

**G-4. No 18 USC §2703(d) minimum-dataset extract procedure.**
**Section/file:** Not addressed
**Investigation scenario:** A 2703(d) order is narrower than a Rule 41 warrant — relevant and material to an ongoing investigation, no probable cause required, no PII over-collection allowed. The receiver is required to produce only what the order authorizes. The chain is integrity-bound but the spec gives no mechanical way to extract a `(tenant_id, customer_id, date_range)` slice that still verifies.
**Evidentiary issue:** 18 USC §2703(d); Stored Communications Act minimization. An over-broad production exposes the institution to a Bivens-style claim from non-target customers and gives defense counsel a Fourth Amendment hook.
**What I'd need to see:** A normative "selective-production verifier mode" — the verifier accepts a `(run_id)` filter list, walks only those runs, and produces a partial-verification report that states explicitly which runs were and were not evaluated. The Merkle inclusion proof per RFC 6962 §2.1.1 is the right tool: I want a Merkle inclusion proof for each produced entry against the day's signed root, so I prove the produced entries were members of the sealed set without producing the whole day. Spec §4.2 has the Merkle root; §7 walks the full day. Add a §7.x "inclusion-proof verification" path.

**G-5. The "AI did not make a decision on this account" negative-evidence proof has no normative spec hook.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §6.3
**Investigation scenario:** Bank-fraud case. Suspect claims he laundered through the LLM-augmented loan-decisioning system, then claims his application was AI-rejected to give the impression he wasn't a customer. I need to prove on the stand that no AI decision was made on a specific account during a window.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 803(7) (absence of record); the institution's "completeness claim" lives in CC8.1 (a SOC document) but the spec doesn't bind it. A defense Daubert challenge attacks the absence-of-evidence inference at exactly that seam.
**What I'd need to see:** A normative `audit.completeness.scope` attribute on a daily marker entry — the institution declares "every AI decision class C for tenant T on day D was captured" under MAC. The verifier reports it; the AUSA cites it. Right now I'm relying on the institution's voluntary CC8.1 declaration, which is impeachable.

**G-6. No grand-jury-readable summary artifact.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §13
**Investigation scenario:** Grand jury, probable cause standard. I need a one-page artifact a lay grand juror can absorb that shows what the AI said and that the record is intact. The juror analogies in §13.1 are fine for trial but they're voice-over, not artifact.
**Evidentiary issue:** Not strictly an FRE issue; it's a presentation gap that affects whether an indictment goes out clean.
**What I'd need to see:** A standardized `verifier --grand-jury-summary` output: one page per run, plain-English, showing the AI's prompt and response, the timestamp, the verifier's PASS, and the four core analogies as captions on a small diagram. Reproducible byte-for-byte so I can hash it as an exhibit.

**G-7. Best-evidence rule answer is incomplete for the case where the LLM token stream is the "original."**
**Section/file:** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §5.2, `docs/litigation-support.md` §4
**Investigation scenario:** Defense argues under FRE 1002 that the LLM's actual token stream is the "original" and the captured JSON is at best a duplicate under FRE 1003. The token stream no longer exists at the provider. Section 5.2 says both forms are originals under FRE 1001(d) but doesn't address the case where the genuine original (the streamed bytes from OpenAI/Anthropic) is gone.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 1002, 1003, 1004. Under 1004(a) the original is admissible if "all the originals are lost or destroyed, and not by the proponent acting in bad faith." The chain captures what the application received, not what the provider sent. A defense expert can argue intermediate transformation.
**What I'd need to see:** Spec §5.2 should explicitly invoke FRE 1004(a) and name the provider attestation captured under `gen_ai.provider_attestation` (§4.4) as the bridge. The provider attestation is the link between provider-side bytes and institution-side capture; spec mentions it as opaque but doesn't litigate-frame it. Litigation-support should give counsel the 1004(a) recital.

**G-8. Daubert "known error rate" answer is qualitative, not quantitative.**
**Section/file:** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §1.1
**Investigation scenario:** Defense expert under FRE 702 demands a numeric false-negative rate. The spec says "no practical attack is known" and lists three layers of compromise. That answers a Frye general-acceptance question; Daubert wants a number.
**Evidentiary issue:** FRE 702; United States v. Vayner, 769 F.3d 125 (2d Cir. 2014), tightened authentication for ESI; defense cites it for novel methods. United States v. Bansal, 663 F.3d 634 (3d Cir. 2011), authenticated bank ESI under 803(6) with quantitative reliability evidence in the record.
**What I'd need to see:** Spec or litigation-support should cite the cryptographic-strength bound directly: HMAC-SHA-256 EUF-CMA at 2^128 queries; Ed25519 at ~2^128 operations; SHA-256 second-preimage at 2^254. The bounds are in §1.3 already; they need to land in the Daubert paragraph as the "error rate" answer. "The probability of undetected tampering is bounded above by 2^-128 under current cryptographic assumptions" is a number a defense expert has to confront, not deflect.

**G-9. No procedure for the institution that has been compromised but the chain hasn't.**
**Section/file:** `docs/incident-response-playbook.md` (referenced but not read), `docs/design/09-threat-model.md` §2.7 (Adversary F)
**Investigation scenario:** Adversary F per the threat model — the SDK process is rooted, attacker has the live session key, produces forward-only forged events. My defendant claims this happened to him. The institution has to prove either (a) it didn't or (b) the forgery window is bounded and identifiable.
**Evidentiary issue:** Spoliation by either side under FRCP 37(e); also a Brady/Giglio question if the prosecution withholds evidence of the compromise window.
**What I'd need to see:** A normative requirement that any host-compromise IR event be itself chain-bound (operational event with `audit.security.host_compromise_window`) so the chain's own record names the bounded window where forward-forgery was possible. Right now the institution's IR record lives outside the chain.

**G-10. MLAT and GDPR Article 48 are mentioned for civil discovery but not for criminal evidence.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §11.1
**Investigation scenario:** Bank's chain data is in Frankfurt. I'm not pursuing civil discovery; I'm running a federal criminal investigation. GDPR Article 48 prohibits direct production to a U.S. court without an MLAT or Hague Evidence Convention basis. The CLOUD Act may or may not apply.
**Evidentiary issue:** GDPR Art. 48; CLOUD Act §103; Microsoft Ireland (United States v. Microsoft Corp., 138 S. Ct. 1186 (2018)) was mooted but the questions remain.
**What I'd need to see:** A criminal-side appendix in `docs/legal-disclosure.md` naming the MLAT path for chain data held in EU regions under §10.15 Pattern A or B, the in-region custodian discipline that satisfies GDPR Art. 49(1)(d) "important reasons of public interest" for criminal cooperation, and the documentation the institution should preserve for a future MLAT request.

### Partials (addressed for civil litigation but thin for criminal)

**P-1. FRE 803(6) regular-course-of-business framing is civil-shaped.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §5.1
The "double hearsay" framing is correct: the chain entry is the record; the AI's statement within is the layered statement. For criminal cross, I need one more sentence — the AI's statement is admissible to show what the bank acted on, not for the truth of the AI's statement. That's the framing that survives Crawford v. Washington, 541 U.S. 36 (2004), in any case where the AI's output is testimonial in character. The spec doesn't go there because Crawford is criminal-only.

**P-2. Privilege and work-product apply to attorney-AI consultation.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §7
Strong on the civil side. The criminal-side concern is different: a prosecution that subpoenas the chain may receive entries the institution claims are privileged. Federal grand jury subpoenas pierce privilege only on a crime-fraud showing; the procedure for in-camera review of `audit.legal_review.*` entries needs a criminal hook.

**P-3. Verifier reproducibility evidence stops at the binary hash.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §10.3
The `verifier.run_completed` event captures binary SHA-256 and operator identity. For criminal trial I'd want the full SLSA provenance, the build's GitHub Actions run-id with timestamps from a time-stamping authority, and the cosign signature's transparency-log inclusion proof from Sigstore Rekor. Today I'd build that case from `docs/supply-chain.md` (referenced) but the litigation-support doc doesn't cite the artifacts by name.

**P-4. Witness-mode verification is documented but not framed for criminal cooperation.**
**Section/file:** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §7 witness-verifier mode
Witness-mode produces `Status: PASS-STRUCTURALLY, key-bound verification skipped` without the IKM. That's the right answer for an institution that won't (or can't, under foreign law) hand the master key to a federal agent. But the spec doesn't address the case where I want to compel disclosure of the IKM under a search warrant. United States v. Bowne, 2010 (no central federal precedent on cryptographic key compulsion; Fricosu and Doe, 11th Cir., are the closest analogues) — the institution's posture under compulsion needs naming, not because the spec resolves the legal question but because the operator needs procedure.

**P-5. Proportionality response in §6.1 reads as civil-only.**
**Section/file:** `docs/litigation-support.md` §6.1
The proportionality framing is FRCP 26(b)(1); not applicable to a criminal Rule 17(c) subpoena, where the Nixon test (United States v. Nixon, 418 U.S. 683 (1974)) governs (relevance, admissibility, specificity). Criminal-side framing should be added.

**P-6. Algorithm rollover commitment for retention-window cases.**
**Section/file:** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §4.3.2; `docs/litigation-support.md` §2.6
Bank-fraud cases tried in 2032 over 2026-captured chain data are the realistic scenario. The 30-day emergency-patch SLA is good. What I need on the stand is "the entry was captured under an algorithm considered secure at the time, contemporaneous verifier output proves chain integrity at the time of capture, and that contemporaneous output is itself preserved." §10.13 names verifier output retention; the linkage to the algorithm-rotation case isn't explicit.

### Nits (clarification asks)

**N-1.** Spec §1.2 says the chain proves "what the AI said." For a criminal case, that elides the question of whether the AI was the proximate actor. Naming the agent process and the human operator who launched the agent is institution-side — fine — but litigation-support should explicitly cite that the chain does NOT establish *mens rea* of any human; that's a separate evidentiary path through 18 USC §1956 (money laundering) or §1344 (bank fraud) elements proof.

**N-2.** §10.14 names RFC 3161 trusted timestamps as "RECOMMENDED but NOT REQUIRED." For high-stakes criminal cases I'd want them required. The forward-commitment language for the v1.x extension is good; the spec should also name a deployment posture under which an institution opts in voluntarily for AI activity touching named regulatory triggers (CTRs, SARs).

**N-3.** `spec/chain-of-custody-v1.md` §10.5 names AWS CloudHSM, Azure Managed HSM, Google Cloud HSM. For a federal criminal exhibit I want the FIPS 140-2 Level 3 certificate number named on the stand. The spec accepts the products; it doesn't name the certificate identifiers. The institution can produce them; I'd rather they were referenced from the spec.

**N-4.** Spec §4.1 inviolate property 7 — canonical form excludes chain-stamp fields. That property is correctly stated. For a witness on the stand explaining MAC self-non-reference, the example would help: "The MAC covers the AI's actual response. The MAC does not cover its own bytes, because that would be circular." Spec is correct; pedagogical clarity for the witness would help.

**N-5.** §10.11 ECOA translation entry has a normative `output_hash` of the customer-facing translation. For a criminal case where the translation itself is alleged to have misrepresented the AI's output (a misrepresentation theory under 18 USC §1014), I'd want the actual translation text retained somewhere — not necessarily in the chain (PII concern is real) — so I can subpoena it. The spec leaves text retention institution-defined; that's correct but it needs a litigation-support cross-reference.

**N-6.** Verifier exit code 1 (FAIL) and exit code 2 (structural error) should be named in the FRE 902(13) certification template (per G-1). An AUSA cross-checking the verifier's output against the certification needs to know what each exit code means.

### Confirmations (testimony I'd give from this spec on the stand)

**C-1. The cryptographic foundation is FIPS-current and Daubert-defensible.**
HMAC-SHA-256 (FIPS 198-1), SHA-256 (FIPS 180-4), Ed25519 (FIPS 186-5), HKDF (RFC 5869), JCS (RFC 8785), Merkle per RFC 6962. Every primitive is NIST-named. I can lay foundation under FRE 702 with citations the defense expert has to acknowledge.

**C-2. The 12-step verification procedure is testable and reproducible.**
Spec §7 with byte-for-byte normative failure-reason strings, the test-vector corpus, and the verifier exit-code contract per §10.12 give me a procedure I can demonstrate live to a jury or a magistrate. United States v. Vayner authentication is met.

**C-3. The three-layer compromise model gives a clean Daubert answer.**
Spec §1.1 names IKM, ledger storage, and HSM as three independent custody layers, with the §1.2 SDK-process compromise as the named fourth class. I can describe the residual-risk picture under cross without retreating from "the chain is integrity-bearing."

**C-4. The constant-time comparison discipline (§10.8 MUST) is the correct cryptographic-engineering posture and survives a Daubert side-channel attack.**
Defense expert challenge "could a timing oracle leak the MAC?" — answer is in the spec text.

**C-5. The HSM custody posture (§10.5) at FIPS 140-2 Level 3 with `sign`-only authority and separation of duties is the financial-services standard and matches what the FFIEC handbook expects.**
FFIEC IT Examination Handbook (Information Security, Sept 2016) is named as informative reference.

**C-6. The §10.7 software-key adapter exclusion (compile-time / packaging exclusion, regulator-visible "plaintext-dev" KMS handle, verifier rejection under `--strict`) closes the production-misconfiguration class.**
A defense theory that "the chain was produced by a development build" is mechanically refutable from the chain entries themselves.

**C-7. The §4.4.1 routing schema lets me reconstruct the agent's decision graph at the moment of decision.**
For a money-laundering investigation where the AI routed between providers, I can rebuild the routing decisions, the circuit-breaker state, and the policy version under integrity binding. That's the evidence picture I need for an 18 USC §1956 element-of-the-offense showing.

**C-8. Spec §4.4 `gen_ai.request.model` and `gen_ai.response.model` MUST requirement closes the silent-vendor-reroute hole.**
Per §4.4.2 `audit.deployment.intent = vendor_reroute_observed` the institution's detection of provider-side rerouting is itself in the chain. That answers the defense theory "OpenAI swapped the model on us."

**C-9. The `verifier.run_completed` operational event is the chain's own audit trail.**
For the cross-examination "how do we know the verifier output is real" question, the event records binary SHA-256, operator, host, and result. Combined with the institution's SLSA build attestation, that's a full custody chain on the verifier itself.

**C-10. Cross-tenant isolation per §4.1 (HKDF info parameter binds tenant_id) and §10.1 (uniqueness enforcement at the registry layer) gives me an isolation argument grounded in cryptography, not in policy.**
For a multi-tenant subpoena dispute where the bank doesn't want to produce other customers' data, the per-tenant `key_fingerprint` is the cryptographic boundary. Opposing counsel cannot override math.

## Bottom line

I would authenticate this evidence in front of a federal grand jury today and walk it through trial under FRE 901(b)(9) and 902(13)/(14) in front of an Article III judge, conditional on three procedural artifacts the spec doesn't ship: (1) a 902(13)/(14) certification template grounded in 28 USC §1746 (G-1, G-3, N-6), (2) a search-warrant execution procedure for federal investigators imaging the receiver's storage (G-2), and (3) a selective-production / inclusion-proof verifier mode for 2703(d) work (G-4). The cryptographic substrate is current, the verification procedure is reproducible and byte-for-byte normative, and the Daubert posture is real. The spec's epistemic-scope discipline (it proves what the AI said and that the record is intact, not whether the AI was correct or compliant) is exactly the discipline a federal expert witness should adopt — it's narrower than a defense lawyer would prefer to attack, and it's defensible.

Where the spec is short for criminal-side work, the gap is procedural rather than cryptographic. The institution's litigation-support doc is heavily civil-shaped (FRCP 26 proportionality, FRCP 37(e) spoliation, GDPR Schrems II for civil discovery). A criminal-side appendix — Rule 17(c) Nixon framing, Rule 41 search warrant procedure, 18 USC §2703(d) minimum-dataset extract, MLAT for cross-border criminal evidence — would close the remaining surface and let an AUSA in the SDNY or EDVA pick this up without inventing procedure on the fly. I'd recommend that appendix as a v1.x deliverable rather than a v1.0 blocker. For now, the chain is good evidence — it's just evidence I'd build my own procedural scaffolding around.
