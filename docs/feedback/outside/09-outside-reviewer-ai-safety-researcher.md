## Headline

The FFIEC chain-of-custody spec provides the integrity substrate AI safety research desperately needs — tamper-evident, production-scale behavioral logs with full provenance. **Tomás's read pass found 25 places where safety research and alignment evaluation can consume chain entries to address questions regulators, executives, and researchers care about: behavioral drift (same model, same input, different output over weeks), hallucination ground truth (labeling false claims for benchmark construction), fairness audits (demographic decision distributions), and red-team episode reconstruction (finding similar failure modes across production deployments).** The spec's integrity claim is airtight for compliance. But the spec does not articulate (and does not need to articulate, per scope) the research-consumption interface, the sampling guarantees for statistical hypothesis testing, the multimodal-AI representation, or the confidence-calibration capture. **Tomás's findings are not deficiencies — they are invitations for research-facing documentation and optional extensions that would make the chain a first-class research substrate without burdening compliance deployments.**

**What the spec enables for safety research.** The per-event HMAC chain + daily Merkle seal means a researcher can trust the logged decisions are what the model actually produced, not a vendor's post-hoc summary or a filtered view. The `gen_ai_parameters` field (sampling parameters, model version, seed) means a researcher can ask "would the model have decided differently if we'd re-run this decision?" and answer it with reproducibility guarantees. The parent_run_id / parent_seq linkage means a researcher can reconstruct multi-turn conversations and agent flows. The audit.* namespace means institutions can attach metadata (decision class, customer impact tier, fairness-sensitive cohort) that enables stratified analysis.

**What research needs that the spec doesn't yet clarify.** Reproducibility bounds (if I re-run the model on the same input with the same seed + temperature, do I get the same output?). Hallucination labels (post-hoc fact-checking records chained to disputed decisions). Confidence calibration (does the model's probability output match its accuracy?). Multimodal inputs/outputs (how does the chain represent image/audio from LLMs that see documents or listen to customer calls?). Long-tail behavior (sampling strategies that don't miss 1-in-100k failure modes). Differential privacy for published statistics (can researchers publish "Model X is 2.3% more accurate than Model Y" without violating customer privacy?). And IRB-approvable de-identification (NIST SP 800-188 standards for when tokenized production logs become "anonymized" for research publication).

## Supporting questions

### Q-1. Behavioral drift detection — does gen_ai_parameters capture seed + sampling sufficient for reproducibility?

Tomás's core research question: **same AI system, same prompt, does output change over weeks?** To answer it, he needs to re-run a decision under the captured parameters and see if the new output diverges from the logged output. The spec captures `gen_ai.response.model` (request and response side) and promises `gen_ai_parameters` is "JCS-canonical JSON of model sampling and reproducibility parameters" (spec §4.4). The RECOMMENDED contents list temperature, top_p, top_k, seed, max_tokens — excellent. But the spec does NOT mandate which fields are required, which are optional, and which vendor-specific parameters (like OpenAI's `logit_bias` or Anthropic's `sampling_preset`) are in scope. Without a normative list, two banks capturing the same model call produce different parameter sets, and a researcher pooling data across institutions can't mechanically extract the reproducibility substrate. **Ask:** Add to spec §4.4 or design/08-gen_ai_parameters.md: "The `gen_ai_parameters` attribute captures reproducibility inputs with this schema (RECOMMENDED — institutions MAY add vendor-specific fields): {\"temperature\": float, \"top_p\": float, \"top_k\": int, \"seed\": string | int (vendor-specific representation; verifier does not interpret), \"max_tokens\": int, \"stop_sequences\": string[], \"presence_penalty\": float, \"frequency_penalty\": float, \"repetition_penalty\": float, \"sampler_impl\": string (e.g., \"openai-python-sdk-1.3.5\" for cross-version reproducibility)}. Fields absent from the JSON are treated as vendor-default. Institutions document their per-vendor sampling-parameter mapping so researchers can reconstruct reproducibility. A research-facing docs/research-guide.md section articulates how to extract `gen_ai_parameters` and re-run a decision for drift analysis."

### Q-2. Silent vendor-side model-swap detection — audit.deployment.intent hints at this, but what's the resolution?

The spec's audit.deployment.intent = "vendor_reroute_observed" (spec §4.4.2) is tantalizingly close to Tomás's NeurIPS 2025 paper on detecting silent model swaps from output distributions. The question: **when a vendor swaps "gpt-4" behind the same model_id (e.g., pointing to gpt-4-0613 then later to gpt-4-1106), can the chain detect it?** The spec captures `gen_ai.response.model` (the vendor's answer for what model served the response), but if the vendor is lying — or if the vendor genuinely doesn't know they're re-routing — how does a researcher detect the swap? Tomás's approach is statistical: measure decision-distribution divergence in a sliding window, flag when KL-divergence crosses a threshold. But this requires the chain to capture enough metadata per-decision (input distribution, decision distribution, token counts, latency) so a researcher can compute the divergence. The spec's `gen_ai_parameters` is part of the answer; so is capturing raw token counts (not just max_tokens budget, but actual tokens used). **Ask:** Add to design/08-gen_ai_parameters.md or create research-guide.md: "For vendor-swap detection, research consumers need: (1) `gen_ai.response.model` as currently captured (the vendor's claimed model). (2) Actual token usage: request token count, response token count (not just max_tokens). (3) Latency percentiles (p50, p95, p99) per model so detection algorithms can spot latency shifts that correlate with swap. (4) A research-facing statistical test procedure that, given N chain entries per (model, week), computes KL divergence in decision distribution and flags week-to-week divergence exceeding a research-determined threshold. Institutions running multi-vendor deployments are encouraged to emit these fields; the chain does not mandate them, but researchers pooling data across institutions benefit when they're present."

### Q-3. Hallucination ground-truth labeling — can post-hoc fact-checking records chain as children?

One of Tomás's lab's biggest projects is HELM-based benchmark construction for hallucination detection. The pipeline: (1) collect real model outputs, (2) fact-check each output (human review + automated fact-checkers), (3) build a labeled dataset (output text + ground-truth label: "hallucination" or "grounded"). Production chain entries contain AI outputs; if banks can append fact-checking labels as chained entries, researchers get real-world hallucination labels at scale. The spec §10.11 ECOA translation is precedent (chained translation events as children of AI decisions via parent_run_id / parent_seq). Same pattern for hallucination labels should work. **Ask:** Create design/09-hallucination-and-correction-chains.md: "When an institution fact-checks an AI-generated claim post-dispute (per customer-dispute-procedures.md §Hallucination cross-check), the cross-check result is already chained as a `fact_verification` entry with `audit.fact_verification.disposition` (confirmed, contradicted, unverifiable). For research purposes, this entry schema is sufficient, and researchers can extract chains with parent linkage to construct labeled hallucination datasets. Institutions publishing research data SHOULD export chains with hallucination-labeled children so researchers can train hallucination classifiers on production data. The tokenization and de-identification procedures (privacy-by-design.md) apply to exported research chains as well; customer decisions are tokenized, but the AI's reasoning (the hallucination target) can be anonymized and released."

### Q-4. Fairness audit replay — can researchers re-bin by demographic cohort given universal redaction?

Fairness audits compare decision distributions across demographic groups (approved rate for applicant A vs applicant B by age, gender, geography). But the spec's universal patterns (privacy-by-design.md) scrub demographics before canonicalization. A researcher asking "were decisions biased against applicants in ZIP code 90210?" can't directly query chain entries binned by ZIP code if the code was redacted. The tension: privacy law (don't retain ZIP codes longer than necessary for the decision) vs fairness audit (need ZIP codes to analyze decisions by geography). **Ask:** Create a research-guide section: "Fairness audit on chain data: The chain's tokenization (privacy-by-design.md) de-identifies customer PII, but institutions can retain a separate, governance-controlled mapping table (institutional, not in the chain) linking customer tokens to demographic attributes needed for fairness audits. This is not a privacy violation — the mapping is an institution-side control document, not part of the chain. Researchers (or the institution's fairness committee) query: 'Show me decision distribution for customer tokens in demographic cohort C.' The mapping lets the query work without exposing PII. The mapping itself is governed by the institution's fairness-audit procedures; external researchers do not see the mapping, only aggregate statistics across cohorts. This pattern is documented in the institution's DPIA and RoPA (privacy-guide.md sections on fairness audit governance)."

### Q-5. Seed + temperature + sampling capture — is this normative per-entry, or institution-defined?

Tomás's read: spec §4.4 says `gen_ai_parameters` is optional and contains "RECOMMENDED contents" including seed and temperature. But an institution deploying the chain could omit all sampling parameters, and a researcher trying to reproduce a decision couldn't. The question is not whether the spec mandates it (it doesn't and shouldn't), but whether the spec clearly articulates the reproducibility consequence of omitting it. **Ask:** Add to spec §4.4 or design/08: "Reproducibility statement: If an institution omits `gen_ai_parameters` (especially seed and temperature), chain entries from that institution cannot be reproduced — a researcher re-running the same prompt on the same model will get different outputs due to stochasticity. This is operationally acceptable (many deployments don't require reproducibility). But for research purposes, institutions interested in enabling drift analysis, fairness audits, and red-team episode reconstruction SHOULD emit `gen_ai_parameters` with temperature and seed. The institution's MRM policy framework (per policy-alignment.md) documents the reproducibility posture: 'Captured reproducibility parameters enable drift detection' or 'Reproducibility not required for this deployment; seed and temperature omitted.' Research-consuming institutions can then filter their input data to include only deployments with reproducibility parameters."

### Q-6. Confidence calibration audit — does the chain capture probability outputs alongside decisions?

Modern LLMs increasingly emit calibrated probabilities (Claude's use_thinking mode with confidence scores, GPT-4 with logits-bias and token probabilities). Does the chain capture these? The spec's `gen_ai.response` namespace references OpenTel GenAI semconv, which does support `gen_ai.response.finish_reason` and could admit probability fields. But the spec text doesn't name them. **Ask:** Clarify in spec §4.4 or design/08: "Optional: `gen_ai.response.finish_reason` (OpenTel standard: 'stop', 'length', 'error', 'other') MAY be emitted per the OTel GenAI semconv. Optional: `gen_ai.response.model_confidence` (float, 0.0 to 1.0) captures the model's stated confidence in the response when available. Optional: `gen_ai.response.logit_bias` (JSON object) captures per-token logit adjustments when used. These are informational; the chain's integrity claim does not depend on their presence. Research consumers can use presence of these fields as a signal of how much confidence calibration data is available in the institution's chain."

### Q-7. Tool-call audit chain — does the chain capture full tool input + output, or just the tool name?

The spec defines `chain_kind = "tool_call"` but does not define a schema for what goes in a tool-call entry. Tomás's question: when an AI agent calls an external API (a database lookup, a web search, a calculator), does the chain capture the tool's input (the query) and the tool's output (the results)? This is a major safety concern — LLM agents calling external APIs in unintended ways. Without full input/output capture, audit trails are incomplete. **Ask:** Create design/10-tool-call-schema.md: "Tool-call chain entries (`chain_kind = 'tool_call'`) carry this schema (RECOMMENDED): {\"audit.tool.name\": string (e.g., 'database_query', 'web_search'), \"audit.tool.input\": object | string (the tool's input; PII is tokenized per privacy-by-design.md), \"audit.tool.output\": object | string (the tool's output; truncated or summarized if >10KB), \"audit.tool.latency_ms\": int, \"audit.tool.status\": enum ('success' | 'timeout' | 'error'), \"audit.tool.error_message\": string | null}. The full input/output enables safety researchers to detect tool-calling patterns the AI is using (which databases, which web domains, what queries), essential for auditing agent behavior in production."

### Q-8. Context window contents — does the chain capture full system prompt + user prompt + few-shot examples?

Reproducibility requires the FULL context, not just the user prompt. If the system prompt or few-shot examples change week-over-week (a common practice for steering behavior), behavioral drift might be due to context changes, not model changes. The spec's `gen_ai_parameters` field could accommodate this, but the spec doesn't clarify whether "retrieval context" and "system-prompt content or content-hash" are normative or optional. **Ask:** Add to design/08: "For drift analysis, chain entries SHOULD capture or reference the full context: (1) System prompt: either the full text (if <5KB) or a cryptographic hash (SHA-256) plus a version identifier in a prompt-version registry the institution maintains. (2) Few-shot examples: if static, a reference to a version-controlled examples file; if dynamic, a hash of the examples set. (3) Retrieved context (RAG): document IDs and content-hashes of the retrieved context, so a researcher can re-retrieve on later dates and see if the context has changed. Institutions enable drift analysis by capturing the full context; institutions that do not capture full context accept that drift analysis may be confounded."

### Q-9. Multi-turn conversation reconstruction — does parent_run_id semantic support agent flows?

The spec defines `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` for parent-child linkage (spec §4.4). For multi-turn agent flows (user message → agent reasoning → tool call → tool result → agent reasoning → final response), does the chain link turns correctly? Tomás's question: given a chain extract, can a researcher mechanically reconstruct the conversation? **Ask:** Create research-guide.md section: "Multi-turn conversation reconstruction: Use the `parent_run_id` / `parent_seq` fields to reconstruct conversation order. Each turn's output becomes the next turn's input. For a conversation with steps [user message → agent reason → tool call → tool result → agent reason → response], the chain captures each step as a separate entry. Entries 1–N of run_id='conv_123' form a DAG (directed acyclic graph) with parent_seq linkages. A researcher can topologically sort the entries and reconstruct the conversation. This is institution-specific: not all institutions emit turn-level granularity. Institutions doing multi-turn Agent AI are encouraged to emit per-turn entries for full reconstruction support."

### Q-10. Prompt-injection attack detection — is this covered by threat model or is it institution-defined?

The spec's threat model (design/09-threat-model.md) covers external wire attackers (A), insiders (B, C, D), vendor compromise (E), and host compromise (F). But prompt injection — adversarial inputs that hijack model behavior — is not in the threat model. The question is not whether the chain defends against prompt injection (it doesn't; that's application-layer detection), but whether the spec clarifies what chain evidence supports post-hoc prompt-injection detection. **Ask:** Add to threat-model.md or create design/11-injection-and-adversarial-input-detection.md: "Prompt-injection attacks are outside the chain's scope (the chain proves what happened, not whether what happened was an attack). But chain evidence supports post-hoc detection: If an institution's AI safety team suspects a prompt-injection attack occurred at time T on customer C, they can extract the chain entry's captured prompt and input, analyze it for injection signatures (prompt-boundary markers, instruction overrides, token-jailbreaks), and correlate with the decision outcome. The chain's integrity proves the prompt was what the model received; the institution's safety tooling detects whether the prompt was malicious. This is a research-friendly design: researchers can build injection-detection classifiers trained on chain-extracted prompts."

### Q-11. Output toxicity and harm classification — can post-hoc labels attach like hallucination labels?

Similar to hallucination labels (Q-3), institutions might want to attach toxicity or harm classifications to chain entries post-capture. A deployed content-moderation AI might flag outputs as "toxic" or "safe" post-inference, and this label should chain. **Ask:** Extend design/09-hallucination-and-correction-chains.md: "Post-hoc harm classification: Institutions deploying content-moderation or fairness-monitoring pipelines that classify AI outputs (toxicity, bias, sensitive-content category) MAY emit chained entries with `chain_kind = 'audit'` and an `audit.output_classification` schema: {\"audit.output_classification.classifier\": string (e.g., 'toxicity_v1.3'), \"audit.output_classification.category\": string (e.g., 'toxic', 'safe', 'unsafe_bias'), \"audit.output_classification.confidence\": float, \"audit.output_classification.timestamp\": RFC3339}. The classifier can be autonomous (a separate ML model) or human-reviewed. These labels support research on toxic-output detection and bias measurement. Institutions publishing research data can export these labeled chains for classifier training."

### Q-12. Statistical hypothesis testing on chain data — what aggregation queries does the spec support?

Tomás wants to assert "Model X is 2.3% more accurate than Model Y on task Z, with p<0.05." This requires N samples per (model, task) cell, aggregation queries across the chain. The spec does not define aggregation — that's outside its scope. But the spec could clarify what sampling guarantees chain data provides. **Ask:** Create research-guide.md section: "Aggregation and statistical testing: Chain data is suitable for hypothesis testing provided institutions sample uniformly. If an institution's sample is biased (e.g., only high-value loans in the chain), statistical claims are invalid. Researchers MUST document their sampling assumptions. The spec does not enforce uniform sampling; institutions define their sampling policy in their control description (§3 of CC8.1). A research paper using chain data from Institution A MUST disclose Institution A's sampling bias (if any) in the paper's data-availability statement. Meta-research question: can researchers pool chains from multiple institutions? Only if the institutions' sampling policies are comparable; a researcher should not pool Institution A's chains (sampled uniformly) with Institution B's chains (sampled on-demand for disputes only)."

### Q-13. Differential privacy for published statistics — can researchers add DP noise before publishing?

If Tomás's lab publishes "analyzed 50,000 loan decisions from Bank X, Model Y is 3.1% more accurate than Model Z," this may be a privacy disclosure under differential privacy theory (an attacker could infer information about specific individuals). But standard DP noising (Laplace mechanism, etc.) is outside the chain's scope. The question: can researchers apply DP transforms to aggregates computed from chain data and still defend the DP claim? **Ask:** Add to research-guide.md: "Privacy-preserving research on chain data: Researchers analyzing chain data for publication MUST consider differential privacy. Options: (1) Aggregate before publication: compute statistics (decision accuracy, model comparison), add Laplace noise proportional to the query sensitivity, then publish the noised aggregate. (2) Use the institution's privacy-store de-identification: after the 7-year retention period, tokens in the chain become irreversible (mapping deleted). Research published on post-7-year de-identified chains has no privacy risk. (3) Federated analysis: researchers do not receive raw chains; instead, they run queries against an institution's secure computation service, which returns noised aggregates. These patterns are documented in the institution's research-data-sharing policy; researchers and institutions collaborate to ensure publications are privacy-safe."

### Q-14. Reproducibility for academic publication — can researchers verify same-seed rerunning works?

Academic papers analyzing chain data need reproducibility statements: "We analyzed X chain entries from Model Y under conditions Z; here are our results. Readers can verify by re-running the model under the same conditions." But can readers actually re-run? Only if the chain captured seed + temperature + system prompt + input. A research paper published without reproducibility guarantees is less credible. **Ask:** Add to research-guide.md: "Reproducibility for papers: When publishing research on chain data, include a reproducibility statement: 'All analyzed chain entries included captured `gen_ai_parameters` (seed, temperature, max_tokens, system prompt hash) and input text (tokenized per privacy-by-design.md). Researchers can request re-run of same inputs on same model to validate results, subject to the institution's research-access policy.' If the chain data lacks reproducibility parameters, state that explicitly: 'This analysis is descriptive (decision distributions, fairness metrics); causal conclusions require reproducibility, which the source chains do not support.'"

### Q-15. Evaluation harness integration — can production chains feed HELM or BIG-bench benchmarks?

HELM and BIG-bench are academic evaluation harnesses that benchmark model behavior across task families. If production chain entries (with captured inputs/outputs) could feed these harnesses, researchers could answer "how does this production model's performance compare to HELM's benchmark?" But the chain does not define a standardized export format for evaluation harnesses. **Ask:** Create design/12-evaluation-harness-interop.md: "Chain-to-harness export: Institutions interested in supporting research can export chain data in a format compatible with HELM and BIG-bench harnesses. Minimum fields per entry: (1) Input (prompt, context, task description). (2) Output (model response). (3) Model identifier and version. (4) Ground truth (if available from the institution's downstream processing). (5) Task tags (for harness routing). An institution that publishes a schema and data export process enables researchers to run HELM evaluation on production data, answering 'how does production performance compare to benchmarks?' This is optional; the chain does not mandate it."

### Q-16. Red-team episode reconstruction — when a red-teamer finds a failure mode, can the chain query for similar instances?

Tomás's red-team work: 'I found a jailbreak prompt that makes Model X produce [harmful output]. Are there similar instances in production?' This requires querying the chain for entries where the output matches a similarity threshold (embedding similarity, token sequence match). The chain does not support similarity search; that's a research-layer tool. But the chain could articulate what export format would enable similarity search. **Ask:** Create research-guide.md section: "Red-team episode reconstruction: When red-teamers identify a failure mode in production via chain analysis, the workflow is: (1) Extract the failure-mode entry from the chain. (2) Compute an embedding of the failure-mode prompt and output using a reference embedding model (e.g., all-MiniLM-L6-v2). (3) Run approximate nearest-neighbor search against embeddings of all entries in the chain to find similar instances. (4) Analyze similar instances for common patterns (input distribution, context, token counts, model state). This workflow is institution-side; the chain provides the data, not the search. Institutions interested in supporting red-team research can pre-compute and index embeddings for all chain entries (stored separately from the chain, not part of the chain MAC). Researchers request 'find entries similar to [failure mode]' and the institution returns matching entries (de-identified, subject to privacy policy)."

### Q-17. A/B test analysis — does audit.deployment.intent = ab_test support per-cohort analysis?

The spec captures `audit.deployment.intent = "ab_test"` (spec §4.4.2) and suggests `experiment_id` for grouping. Perfect for fairness research — Tomás wants to analyze 'Model A vs Model B per demographic cohort C: which model is fairer?' **Ask:** Add to research-guide.md: "A/B test analysis: Chain entries tagged with `audit.deployment.intent = 'ab_test'` can be grouped by `experiment_id` and analyzed per cohort. Query: 'For experiment_id='mobile_app_v3.1', show decision distribution (approve/deny rate) by demographic cohort.' This is powerful for validating fairness interventions: institutions can A/B test a new decision logic and measure fairness improvements per cohort in near-real-time using chain data. The institution's demograhic-cohort mapping (per Q-4, a separate governance-controlled table) enables the per-cohort analysis."

### Q-18. Long-tail behavior detection — sampling and aggregation strategies for 1-in-100k rare events?

Most sampling strategies lose tail behavior. Tomás needs to find failure modes that occur once per 100k decisions. If chain events are sampled (e.g., 10% sample to reduce storage), rare events disappear. The spec defines sampling is off-scope, but could clarify. **Ask:** Create research-guide.md section: "Long-tail behavior and rare events: If an institution's chain uses sampling (e.g., capturing every Nth event), rare behaviors are underrepresented. Institutions interested in enabling tail-behavior research SHOULD retain all events (no sampling), or oversample failure-cases (e.g., 100% sample when decision='deny', 10% when decision='approve'). The institution documents its sampling strategy in CC8.1; researchers can then assess whether their findings are representative. A research paper analyzing production chains MUST state: 'All decisions analyzed' (no sampling) or 'Oversampled failure cases by 10x' (accounting for selection bias in results)."

### Q-19. Model-card / evaluation-card linkage — can chains link to published model documentation?

Model cards (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) document model behavior, training data, and known limitations. If chain entries linked to the model-card version corresponding to the model at decision time, researchers could cross-reference production behavior against published documentation. **Ask:** Add to spec §4.4 or design/08: "Optional: `gen_ai.model_card_url` and `gen_ai.model_card_version` MAY be emitted on model-call entries. These fields reference the model's published documentation (model card, technical report, evaluation card) at the version deployed at decision time. Researchers can then cross-reference: 'The model card says Model X has 92% accuracy on task Y; production chain data shows 91.3% — is there a gap?' This linkage is informational; the chain's integrity does not depend on it."

### Q-20. Reasoning-trace capture — modern reasoning models (o1, extended-thinking) emit long reasoning. Storage and research implications?

Claude 4 with extended thinking, o1, and similar models emit internal reasoning traces (often 10k+ tokens). Do these go in the chain? Storage cost is high; research value is enormous. The spec does not address reasoning traces. **Ask:** Create design/13-reasoning-trace-capture.md: "Reasoning-trace capture is optional and institution-defined. Options: (1) Full capture: the entire reasoning trace goes in `audit.reasoning_trace` (large, storage-intensive, valuable for interpretability research). (2) Summary capture: a compressed summary (tokenized key insights, decision points) goes in `audit.reasoning_trace_summary` (smaller, still useful). (3) No capture: reasoning traces are not logged (preserves privacy, reduces storage, but limits interpretability research). Institutions interested in supporting interpretability research opt for (1) or (2); institutions prioritizing efficiency opt for (3). The institution's CC8.1 documents the choice."

### Q-21. Multimodal AI — images, audio, video inputs/outputs from multimodal models?

As LLMs incorporate vision (GPT-4V, Claude 3.5 with vision), audio (Anthropic's audio models), the chain's text-centric design needs clarification. How does the chain represent image inputs? As base64? As hashes? As references to object-store? **Ask:** Create design/14-multimodal-representation.md: "Multimodal AI in the chain: When a model processes images, audio, or video, the chain represents the input as: (1) Media hash (SHA-256 of the media bytes) when the media is large (>100KB). (2) Media reference (object-store path, e.g., 's3://bucket/image_12345.jpg') when the media is externally stored. (3) Media embedding (vector representation in `audit.modality.embedding`) for similarity search. The decision output (text) is captured normally. This design lets researchers analyze 'what visual features drove this decision?' without forcing multi-GB chain files. Institutions deploying multimodal AI document their media-representation choice in CC8.1."

### Q-22. Embedding capture — can chains store or reference embeddings of inputs/outputs for similarity search?

Related to Q-16 (red-team similarity search), storing embeddings enables efficient nearest-neighbor queries. Should the chain capture embeddings, or should they be computed separately (chain agnostic)? **Ask:** Create design/08-gen_ai_parameters.md section: "Optional: `audit.embedding.input_embedding` and `audit.embedding.output_embedding` (vectors in a standard format, e.g., base64-encoded float32 array or reference to an embedding-store service) MAY be emitted. These support similarity search for red-teaming. If emitted, the institution specifies the embedding model (e.g., 'all-MiniLM-L6-v2') so researchers know how to search. Embeddings are NOT integrity-bound (they're informational); the chain's MAC covers the text, not the embeddings."

### Q-23. Safety-research-specific event types — should there be a chain_kind for safety observations?

The spec defines chain_kind = audit, model_call, tool_call, routing, translation, operational. Should there be a safety_observation type for documenting when an institution's safety monitoring system flags an entry (toxicity detector, jailbreak detector, harmful-output classifier)? **Ask:** Create design/15-safety-observation-chain.md: "Optional: Institutions deploying safety-monitoring pipelines (toxicity detection, jailbreak detection, harmful-output classification) MAY emit chained entries with `chain_kind = 'safety_observation'` (a v1.1 candidate) capturing: {\"audit.safety.detector_type\": string (e.g., 'toxicity', 'jailbreak', 'bias'), \"audit.safety.risk_score\": float (0–1), \"audit.safety.risk_category\": string, \"audit.safety.parent_run_id\": the entry being flagged}. This enables research on safety-detector performance and calibration. For v1.0, institutions use `chain_kind = 'audit'` with `audit.safety_observation.*` namespace; v1.1 may introduce a dedicated enum value."

### Q-24. Ethics review / IRB compliance — does chain's de-identification support IRB-approvable research?

Research using human-subjects data requires IRB approval. Does the chain's tokenization (privacy-by-design.md) satisfy de-identification standards (NIST SP 800-188)? **Ask:** Add to privacy-guide.md or create research-guide.md section: "IRB compliance for chain research: NIST SP 800-188 defines de-identification as reducing identifiability through removal or transformation of direct identifiers and quasi-identifiers. The chain's tokenization (privacy-by-design.md) removes direct identifiers (customer ID → token). For quasi-identifiers (age, ZIP code, loan amount), the institution's de-identification policy determines whether they're retained, redacted, or aggregated. Researchers planning to analyze chain data and publish MUST coordinate with the institution's IRB to confirm the de-identification approach satisfies the IRB's standards. After the 7-year retention period ends, the privacy-store mapping is deleted and tokens become irreversibly anonymized — post-7-year research is automatically IRB-exempt. During retention, researchers must obtain informed consent or rely on IRB waiver (e.g., minimal risk, no direct contact with subjects)."

### Q-25. Confidence in vendor-provided attestations — how does the chain handle provider attestations?

Some vendors (Anthropic, OpenAI) provide cryptographic attestations that the model generated the response. The spec's `gen_ai.provider_attestation` field (spec §4.4) captures the attestation opaquely. But how does a researcher validate it? Is there a standard format? **Ask:** Clarify in design/08: "Provider attestation validation (research consumer perspective): The `gen_ai.provider_attestation` field is captured byte-for-byte but not validated by the chain (validation is institution-side). Researchers consuming chains with attestations MUST obtain the vendor's public key and validation procedure from the vendor's documentation. If the chain includes attestation, a researcher can (1) verify the attestation with the vendor's key, (2) confirm the model produced the output, (3) cross-reference with the institution's model version. This adds a second layer of integrity: the chain proves institution custody, the attestation proves vendor origin. The spec does not name a standard attestation format; vendors define their own. An institution publishing research chains with attestations SHOULD include the vendor's validation procedure in the research package."
